## .facts

### fact: NCI — any suspicious lesion warrants biopsy by local excision

- **fact**: the NCI PDQ states a biopsy, preferably by local excision, should be performed for any suspicious lesion, and that suspicious lesions should never be shaved off or cauterized.
- **verbatim**: "A biopsy, preferably by local excision, should be performed for any suspicious lesions. Suspicious lesions should never be shaved off or cauterized. An experienced pathologist should examine the specimens to allow for microstaging."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.gov/types/skin/hp/melanoma-treatment-pdq
- **tier**: regulator/government (NCI PDQ, peer-reviewed board summary)

### fact: NCI — early change signs that trigger concern in a nevus

- **fact**: per the NCI, early signs in a nevus that suggest malignant change include darker or variable discoloration, itch, an increase in size or development of satellite lesions, and ulceration or bleed (later signs).
- **verbatim**: "Early signs in a nevus that would suggest a malignant change include: Darker or variable discoloration. Itching. An increase in size or the development of satellite lesions. Ulceration or bleeding (later signs)."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.gov/types/skin/hp/melanoma-treatment-pdq
- **tier**: regulator/government (NCI PDQ)

### fact: the "ugly duckling" sign — the outlier lesion

- **fact**: the ugly duckling sign flags a lesion that stands out as an outlier — one that changes or differs from the patient's other lesions — as a prompt for closer evaluation.
- **verbatim**: "a screening examination to look for any lesion that stands out because of being dark, different, or changing ... recognition of outlier (\"ugly-duckling\") lesions"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4230266/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (Dermatology Practical & Conceptual, Goldsmith 2014)

### fact: ABCDE is a spot-evaluation tool, not a screen; the outlier search comes first

- **fact**: the ABCDE criteria are best used as a per-lesion spot evaluation (and to compare similar lesions), whereas the step that governs sensitivity is the search for a lesion that stands out.
- **verbatim**: "the role of the ABCDE criteria is not as a screening approach, as they have been utilized, but as a spot evaluation, and the criteria can also help to assess the level of concern when comparing similar lesions"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4230266/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (Dermatology Practical & Conceptual)

### fact: dermoscopy score cutoff — ABCD rule of dermoscopy total score > 4.75

- **fact**: the ABCD rule of dermoscopy uses a total dermoscopy score threshold of > 4.75 as the standard cutoff to flag a lesion as suspicious for melanoma; higher cutoffs (e.g. > 5.45) raise specificity at the cost of sensitivity.
- **verbatim**: "ABCD at > 4.75 | ... | 10 | 4242 (816) | 0.81 (0.67 to 0.90) | 0.72 ... | ABCD at > 5.45 | 4 | 1203 (155) | 0.78 ... | 0.93 ..."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517096/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: dermoscopy score cutoff — 7-point checklist positive at >= 3

- **fact**: the 7-point checklist flags a lesion for excision/referral at a score of >= 3 (its standard positivity threshold); a lower threshold of >= 2 raises sensitivity but lowers specificity.
- **verbatim**: "7PCL at >= 3 | ... | 11 | 3408 (798) | 0.80 (0.63 to 0.91) | 0.67 (0.51 to 0.80)"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517096/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: threshold governs the sensitivity/specificity trade-off

- **fact**: across visual-inspection algorithms, the choice of positivity threshold trades sensitivity against specificity — e.g. the 7-point checklist rose from specificity 65% at >= 2 to 98% at >= 4, while its sensitivity fell from 92% to 25%.
- **verbatim**: "7‐point checklist at >= 2 | ... | 92% (62 to 1.00) | 65% (58 to 72) ... 7‐point checklist at >= 4 | ... | 25% (07 to 57) | 98% (96 to 100)"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492463/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)
