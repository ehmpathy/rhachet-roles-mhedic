## .facts

### fact: what the letters A-B-C-D-E stand for

- **fact**: the ABCDE rule denotes Asymmetry, irregular Borders, more than one or uneven distribution of Colour, a large (greater than 6 mm) Diameter, and Evolution of moles.
- **verbatim**: "ABCDE, Asymmetry, irregular Borders, more than one or uneven distribution of Colour, or a large (greater than 6mm) Diameter, Evolution of moles"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5353325/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (BMJ Open systematic review, Harrington 2017)

### fact: ABCDE is the most common naked-eye clinical prediction rule

- **fact**: among clinical prediction rules for melanoma in ambulatory care, the most commonly applied clinical (naked-eye) rule was the ABCDE rule.
- **verbatim**: "The most commonly applied clinical CPR was the ABCDE rule (five studies)"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5353325/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (BMJ Open systematic review)

### fact: ABCDE clinical rule single-study accuracy at cut-point >= 2

- **fact**: in individual studies from the review, the ABCDE clinical rule at cut-point >= 2 showed sensitivity 85.0% with specificity 44.5% in one cohort and sensitivity 47.3% with specificity 56.0% in another — a demonstration of wide variability.
- **verbatim**: "ABCDE clinical rule (cut-point >=2) Se: 85.0 Sp: 44.5 ... ABCDE clinical rule (cut-point of >=2) Se: 47.3 Sp: 56.0"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5353325/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (BMJ Open systematic review)

### fact: pooled in-person (A)BCD(E) accuracy (cochrane meta-analysis)

- **fact**: in a cochrane systematic review of visual inspection for melanoma, in-person use of the (A)BCD(E) algorithm (positive at >= 2 characteristics present) across 6 datasets (5501 lesions, 654 melanomas) had pooled sensitivity 83% and pooled specificity 88%.
- **verbatim**: "(A)BCD(E) | 6 | 5501 (654) | 83% (75 to 88) | 88% (64 to 97) | 36.6 (7.94 to 168)"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492463/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review, Dinnes 2018)

### fact: image-based ABCD(E) accuracy is markedly lower

- **fact**: when the ABCD(E) algorithm was applied to images rather than in-person (2 datasets, 153 lesions, 46 melanomas), pooled sensitivity fell to 53% and specificity to 71%.
- **verbatim**: "ABCD(E) | 2 | 153 (46) | 53% (37 to 70) | 71% (45 to 88) | 2.87 (0.93 to 8.79)"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492463/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: in-person visual inspection beats image-based

- **fact**: the review found accuracy generally much higher for in-person diagnosis than image-based evaluation, with a relative diagnostic odds ratio of 8.54.
- **verbatim**: "Accuracy was generally much higher for in‐person diagnosis compared to image‐based evaluations (relative diagnostic odds ratio of 8.54, 95% CI 2.89 to 25.3, P < 0.001)."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492463/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: sensitivity/specificity trade-off shifts along the clinical pathway

- **fact**: highest sensitivity (92.4%) with lowest specificity (79.7%) occurred in patients with limited prior work-up, while lesions already selected for excision showed lower sensitivity (76.7%) but much higher specificity (95.7%).
- **verbatim**: "the highest sensitivity (92.4%, 95% CI 26.2% to 99.8%) and lowest specificity (79.7%, 95% CI 73.7% to 84.7%) observed in participants with limited prior testing ... sensitivity 76.7%, 95% CI 61.7% to 87.1%) and specificity 95.7%, 95% CI 89.7% to 98.3%) for lesions selected for excision"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492463/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: documented limit — visual inspection alone misses melanomas

- **fact**: the review's authors conclude that melanomas will be missed if visual inspection is used on its own, and that published algorithms did not clearly improve accuracy.
- **verbatim**: "the evidence suggests that melanomas will be missed if visual inspection is used on its own ... Although published algorithms do not appear to improve accuracy, there is insufficient evidence to suggest that the 'no algorithm' approach should be preferred in all settings."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492463/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: no-algorithm in-person naked-eye baseline

- **fact**: in-person naked-eye assessment with no formal algorithm (21 datasets, 19,330 lesions, 1076 melanomas) had pooled sensitivity 78% and specificity 93%.
- **verbatim**: "No algorithm | 21 | 19,330 (1076) | 78% (68 to 85) | 93% (88 to 96) | 46.2 (21.9 to 97.5)"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492463/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)
