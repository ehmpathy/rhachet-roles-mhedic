## .facts — danger + incidence

### fact: melanoma — less common, more dangerous, spreads if not caught early

- **fact**: melanoma is much less common than BCC/SCC but more dangerous because it is much more likely to spread to other parts of the body if not found and treated early.
- **verbatim**: "Melanoma is much less common than some other types of skin cancers. But melanoma is more dangerous because it's much more likely to spread to other parts of the body if not found and treated early."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/melanoma-skin-cancer/about/what-is-melanoma.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society)

### fact: melanoma US incidence and mortality estimates (2026)

- **fact**: the ACS estimates for 2026 are about 112,000 new melanomas and about 8,510 melanoma deaths in the US; lifetime melanoma risk is about 3% (1 in 33) for White people, 0.1% for Black people, 0.5% for Hispanic people.
- **verbatim**: "About 112,000 new melanomas will be diagnosed (about 65,400 in men and 46,600 in women). About 8,510 people are expected to die of melanoma (about 5,500 men and 3,010 women). ... the lifetime risk of getting melanoma is about 3% (1 in 33) for White people, 0.1% (1 in 1,000) for Black people, and 0.5% (1 in 200) for Hispanic people."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/melanoma-skin-cancer/about/key-statistics.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society)

## .facts — subtypes + the ABCDE anchor

### fact: melanoma subtypes and their growth behavior

- **fact**: the most common melanoma is superficial spreading (~7 in 10, grows outward first); nodular (~2 in 10) grows down into deeper layers early and is often more advanced when found; lentigo maligna (older people, sun-exposed, slow); acral lentiginous (palms, soles, under nails; a large share of melanomas in darker skin tones).
- **verbatim**: "Superficial spreading melanoma: This type makes up about 7 in 10 melanomas ... Nodular melanoma: This type accounts for about 2 in 10 skin melanomas ... Nodular melanomas tend to grow down into deeper layers of the skin fairly early, so they're often at a more advanced stage ... Acral lentiginous melanoma ... starts in areas that don't get a lot of sun exposure, such as the palms of the hands, soles of the feet, or under the nails."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/melanoma-skin-cancer/about/what-is-melanoma.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society)

### fact: ABCDE applies to melanoma; melanoma is "the most serious skin cancer"

- **fact**: melanoma can grow rapidly and spread beyond the skin, can appear within a mole a person already has or as a new dark spot different from other moles, and the ABCDEs help find it early; it is described as the most serious skin cancer with good outcomes when caught early.
- **verbatim**: "Known as 'the most serious skin cancer' ... Can grow rapidly and spread beyond the skin. Can appear within a mole that you already have or show up as a new dark spot that looks different from the rest of your moles. ABCDEs of melanoma can help you find it early. Outcome can be good when diagnosed early and properly treated."
- **url**: https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/types/common
- **tier**: professional society (American Academy of Dermatology)

## .facts — stage-survival (why early detection is the whole thesis)

### fact: melanoma survival collapses as it spreads — localized >99%, distant 35%

- **fact**: SEER 5-year relative survival for melanoma of the skin (diagnosed 2015–2021) is >99% localized, 76% regional, 35% distant, and 95% all stages combined.
- **verbatim**: "Localized >99% ... Regional 76% ... Distant 35% ... All SEER stages combined 95%"
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/melanoma-skin-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/survival-rates-for-melanoma-skin-cancer-by-stage.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society, SEER/NCI data)

## .facts — the amelanotic / "does-not-fit-the-rules" pitfall

### fact: some melanomas do not fit ABCDE — the ugly-duckling sign matters

- **fact**: the most important warning sign is a new or changing spot, or a spot that looks different from all the others (the "ugly duckling sign"); some melanomas do not fit the ABCDE rules, and color may include pink, red, white, or blue and be smaller than 6 mm — so a non-classic or non-pigmented lesion is not reassurance.
- **verbatim**: "Another important sign is a spot that looks different from all of the other spots on your skin. (This is sometimes known as \"the ugly duckling sign.\") ... The color is not the same all over and may include different shades of brown or black, or sometimes with patches of pink, red, white, or blue. ... although melanomas can sometimes be smaller than this. ... Some melanomas don't fit these rules."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/melanoma-skin-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/signs-and-symptoms.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society)

### fact: hidden melanoma — nails, palms/soles, eye, mucosa

- **fact**: a small portion of melanomas start where the sun does not reach and look different: a dark nail streak (acral), dark irregular areas on palms/soles (acral), a dark spot in the iris (uveal), or dark/irregular areas in mouth, nose, or genitals (mucosal).
- **verbatim**: "Under a fingernail or toenail (acral melanoma): May appear as a dark line or streak in the nail. On the palms or soles (acral melanoma): May appear as a dark, irregular areas. In the eye (uveal melanoma): May appear as a dark spot in the colored part of the eye (iris). In the mouth, nose, and genitals (mucosal melanoma): May develop as dark spots or irregular areas in these tissues."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/melanoma-skin-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/signs-and-symptoms.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society)
