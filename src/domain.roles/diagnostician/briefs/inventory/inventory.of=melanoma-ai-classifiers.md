## .facts — accuracy + failure modes (cochrane review PMC6517294, Freeman 2020)

### fact: AI-based smartphone classifier sensitivity varies widely and can be very low

- **fact**: across four artificial-intelligence-based apps that classified lesion images, sensitivities ranged from 7% to 73% and specificities from 37% to 94% (332 lesions, 86 melanomas across two studies).
- **verbatim**: "Across the four artificial intelligence‐based applications that classified lesion images (photographs) as melanomas (one application) or as high risk or 'problematic' lesions (three applications) using a pre‐programmed algorithm, sensitivities ranged from 7% (95% CI 2% to 16%) to 73% (95% CI 52% to 88%) and specificities from 37% (95% CI 29% to 46%) to 94% (95% CI 87% to 97%)."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517294/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: store-and-forward (dermatologist review) app had high sensitivity, low specificity

- **fact**: the single app that used store-and-forward review of images by a dermatologist had sensitivity 98% and specificity 30%.
- **verbatim**: "The single application using store‐and‐forward review of lesion images by a dermatologist had a sensitivity of 98% (95% CI 90% to 100%) and specificity of 30% (95% CI 22% to 40%)."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517294/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: documented failure mode — high rate of 'unevaluable' images, including missed melanomas

- **fact**: test failures (images classed as 'unevaluable' and excluded) ranged from 2% to 18% of lesions; at least one melanoma was classed as unevaluable in three of the four app evaluations.
- **verbatim**: "The number of test failures ... ranged from 3 to 31 (or 2% to 18% of lesions analysed). ... At least one melanoma was classed as unevaluable in three of the four application evaluations."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517294/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: authors' conclusion — AI apps risk missed melanomas

- **fact**: the review concluded AI-based smartphone apps had not yet demonstrated sufficient accuracy and carry a high likelihood of missed melanomas.
- **verbatim**: "Smartphone applications using artificial intelligence‐based analysis have not yet demonstrated sufficient promise in terms of accuracy, and they are associated with a high likelihood of missing melanomas."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517294/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: selection bias inflates reported app sensitivity

- **fact**: all included lesions had already been selected for excision in a dermatology clinic, a more severe spectrum than community use, which artificially raises the sensitivity relative to real-world smartphone use.
- **verbatim**: "This is not a representative spectrum of lesions that would be observed in daily life but rather a highly selected sample of participants. These participants would have already presented to a doctor ... which will artificially increase the sensitivity of the test in comparison to use by smartphone users in general."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517294/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

## .facts — regulatory status (regulator, primary)

### fact: MelaFind is an FDA premarket-approved (PMA) class III melanoma-detection device

- **fact**: the FDA PMA database lists device MELAFIND (optical diagnostic device for melanoma detection), PMA number P090012, applicant Strata Skin Sciences, with a decision date of 11/01/2011 — a formally premarket-approved medical device, not an unregulated consumer app.
- **verbatim**: "Device: MELAFIND | Generic Name: Optical diagnostic device for melanoma detection | PMA Number: P090012 | Applicant: Strata Skin Sciences, Inc. | Decision Date: 11/01/2011 | Product Code: OYD | Advisory Committee: General & Plastic Surgery"
- **url**: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMA/pma.cfm?id=P090012
- **tier**: regulator (FDA CDRH PMA database, primary)

### fact: contrast — consumer AI apps in the review were not evaluated as approved devices

- **fact**: the apps assessed in the cochrane review were consumer/community-intended smartphone apps used outside their intended setting; the review drew no practice implications given the paucity and low quality of the evidence, which distinguishes them from a formally approved device such as MelaFind.
- **verbatim**: "Given the paucity of evidence and low methodological quality of existing studies, it is not possible to draw any implications for practice."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517294/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)
