## .facts

### fact: SCC is ~20% of skin cancers, more likely than BCC to spread

- **fact**: SCC is about 2 of 10 skin cancers; it can usually be removed, but is much more likely than BCC to grow into deeper skin layers and spread to other parts of the body.
- **verbatim**: "About 2 out of 10 skin cancers are squamous cell carcinomas ... Squamous cell cancers can usually be removed completely (or treated in other ways), although they are much more likely than basal cell cancers to grow into deeper layers of skin and spread to other parts of the body."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/basal-and-squamous-cell-skin-cancer/about/what-is-basal-and-squamous-cell.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society)

### fact: SCC appearance — rough/scaly patch, dome growth, or non-healing sore

- **fact**: SCC often looks like a rough scaly patch, a firm dome-shaped growth, or a non-healing sore; it can arise within a precancerous actinic keratosis, and can spread or return after treatment.
- **verbatim**: "Can develop within a precancerous skin growth called an actinic keratosis (AK) ... Often looks like a rough and scaly patch, firm and dome-shaped growth, or a non-healing sore ... Can spread or return after treatment."
- **url**: https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/types/common
- **tier**: professional society (American Academy of Dermatology)
