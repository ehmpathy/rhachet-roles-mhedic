## .facts

### fact: MCC is rare but one of the most dangerous skin cancers

- **fact**: MCC is much less common than other skin cancers but one of the most dangerous; it grows quickly and is much more likely than BCC/SCC to spread, and can be hard to treat once it has spread.
- **verbatim**: "MCC is much less common than most other types of skin cancer, but it's one of the most dangerous types. It's much more likely than common skin cancers (squamous and basal cell skin cancers) to spread to other parts of the body, and it can be hard to treat if it has spread. ... It tends to grow quickly and can spread to other parts of the body."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/merkel-cell-skin-cancer/about/what-is-merkel-cell-carcinoma.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society, with ASCO review)

### fact: MCC appearance — fast-growing firm pink/red/purple painless lump

- **fact**: MCC tumors often look like firm, pink, red, or purple lumps that usually don't hurt but grow quickly and can ulcerate; they most often start on sun-exposed skin (the face is the most common site).
- **verbatim**: "Merkel cell tumors often look like firm, pink, red, or purple lumps or bumps on the skin. They usually don't hurt, but they tend to grow quickly and can sometimes open up as ulcers or sores. ... most often they start on skin that's exposed to the sun, like the face (the most common site), neck, and arms."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/merkel-cell-skin-cancer/about/what-is-merkel-cell-carcinoma.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society, with ASCO review)

### fact: MCC is ~3,000 US cases/yr, rising, mostly older White men

- **fact**: about 3,000 people are diagnosed with MCC in the US each year; the number has been rising quickly; most patients are older than 70, men more than women, and more than 9 of 10 are White.
- **verbatim**: "About 3,000 people are diagnosed with Merkel cell carcinoma in the United States each year. The number of people diagnosed with Merkel cell carcinoma each year has been rising quickly over the past few decades. ... Most Americans diagnosed with Merkel cell carcinoma are older than age 70. Men are more likely to have MCC than women. More than 9 out of 10 Americans diagnosed with Merkel cell carcinoma are White people."
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/merkel-cell-skin-cancer/about/key-statistics.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society, with ASCO review)

### fact: Merkel cell survival is far worse than melanoma at every stage

- **fact**: SEER 5-year relative survival for MCC (diagnosed 2015–2021) is 79% localized, 66% regional, 31% distant, and 69% all stages combined — markedly below melanoma even for localized disease (79% vs >99%).
- **verbatim**: "Localized 79% ... Regional 66% ... Distant 31% ... All SEER stages combined 69%"
- **url**: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/merkel-cell-skin-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/survival-rates.html
- **tier**: professional (American Cancer Society, SEER/NCI data)
