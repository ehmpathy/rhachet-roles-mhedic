## .facts

### fact: teledermatology accuracy for any skin cancer (photographic images)

- **fact**: for the correct diagnosis of lesions as malignant (any skin cancer) via photographic images, summary sensitivity was 94.9% and summary specificity was 84.3% (4 studies).
- **verbatim**: "For the correct diagnosis of lesions as malignant using photographic images, summary sensitivity was 94.9% (95% confidence interval (CI) 90.1% to 97.4%) and summary specificity was 84.3% (95% CI 48.5% to 96.8%) (from four studies)."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517019/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: evidence weaker/more variable for melanoma specifically

- **fact**: for detection of invasive melanoma or atypical intraepidermal melanocytic variants, both sensitivity and specificity were more variable — sensitivities from 59% to 100% and specificities from 30% to 100%.
- **verbatim**: "For the detection of invasive melanoma or atypical intraepidermal melanocytic variants both sensitivities and specificities were more variable. Sensitivities ranged from 59% (95% CI 42% to 74%) to 100% (95% CI 48% to 100%) and specificities from 30% (95% CI 22% to 40%) to 100% (95% CI 93% to 100%)"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517019/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: teledermatology concordance with in-person diagnosis appears similar but data are scarce

- **fact**: limited comparative data suggested similar diagnostic accuracy between teledermatology and in-person diagnosis by a dermatologist, but the data were too scarce to draw firm conclusions.
- **verbatim**: "Limited comparative data suggested similar diagnostic accuracy between teledermatology assessment and in‐person diagnosis by a dermatologist; however, data were too scarce to draw firm conclusions."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517019/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: referral concordance — high for action lesions, more variable for reassurance

- **fact**: against a face-to-face reference, teledermatology showed good agreement for lesions that warrant positive action (sensitivities over 90%), but more variable specificity (57% to 100%) for lower-concern lesions, i.e. remote assessment more often recommends excision/referral/follow-up than the in-person decision.
- **verbatim**: "Referral accuracy data comparing teledermatology against a face‐to‐face reference standard suggested good agreement for lesions considered to require some positive action by face‐to‐face assessment (sensitivities of over 90%). For lesions considered of less concern ... teledermatology specificities ranging from 57% (95% CI 39% to 73%) to 100% (95% CI 86% to 100%), suggesting that remote assessment is more likely recommend excision, referral or follow‐up compared to in‐person decisions."
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517019/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: authors' conclusion — good for triage, but evidence base still lacking

- **fact**: teledermatology can correctly identify the majority of malignant lesions and is likely useful for triage, but the evidence base to support accurate diagnosis and primary-to-secondary-care triage is not yet sufficient.
- **verbatim**: "our results suggest that teledermatology can correctly identify the majority of malignant lesions ... Despite the increasing use of teledermatology on an international level, the evidence base to support its ability to accurately diagnose lesions and to triage lesions from primary to secondary care is lacking"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517019/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)

### fact: applicability caveat — images acquired in specialist settings, not primary care

- **fact**: most studies recruited from secondary-care/specialist clinics with images acquired by dermatologists or specialist imaging units, not the primary/community settings where teledermatology is more likely used.
- **verbatim**: "the recruitment of participants from secondary care settings or specialist clinics rather than from primary or community‐based settings in which teledermatology is more likely to be used and due to the acquisition of lesion images by dermatologists or in specialist imaging units rather than by primary care clinicians"
- **url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517019/
- **tier**: peer-reviewed (cochrane systematic review)
