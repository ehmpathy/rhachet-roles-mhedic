## .the same-day principle

the same-day tier sits between two floors. a scenario lands here when **either**:

1. its worst-case bound worsens over **days** (not hours) — slow enough to skip the emergency
   floor, fast enough that a wait of a week is a real risk, **or**
2. it is a time-critical branch that *leans benign* but is **not cleared** — the emergency floor
   from `7.1` is "emergency-until-cleared", and the clinician's perfusion + neuro exam is the gate
   that lifts it. that exam is a same-day errand: the sooner it clears the ATE/cord branches, the
   sooner the floor drops.

## .the same-day tier

**the same-day / near-term window is: seen within 24–48 hours.**

- the **clearance exam** drives the window: a clinician's perfusion check (clears B16 ATE) + neuro
  check (clears B13 cord) is what lifts the emergency-until-cleared floor. until it happens, the
  time-critical branches stay live; the sooner within 24–48h, the sooner the floor drops.
- the **NSAID-safety exam** rides the same visit: a baseline renal panel before continued
  robenacoxib is the gate the `6.3` care conflict names (the renal harm-vector). a persistent
  limp on onsior past a few days should not continue un-checked.
- the **anorexia clock** can pull the window **sooner**: if miki's appetite drops, B18's clock
  starts, and same-day becomes same-hours (see escalate-sooner below).

> the case *leans* benign (the limp preceded onsior and onsior relieves it — a dose-reversible
> inflammatory picture). that lean is exactly why this is a same-day case, not an emergency one —
> but "leans benign" is not "cleared", so the 24–48h clearance exam is what settles it.

## .the escalate-sooner signs (what lifts a same-day case to emergency)

seek care sooner — treat as the emergency floor from `7.1` — if any of these appear:

- **a cold or limp leg, a bluish / pale paw pad, or absent pulses** → ATE onset (B16); go now.
- **a leg dragged, or held at an odd/awkward angle** → acute cord or fracture event (B13/B12).
- **labored or open-mouth breath, or sudden collapse** → ATE / cardiac decompensation (B16).
- **appetite loss that persists past a day or two** → the hepatic-lipidosis clock has started
  (B18); the same-day window collapses to same-hours.
- **the limp worsens rather than eases on onsior** → the dose-reversible read fails; the
  structural branches climb, and the exam should not wait.
