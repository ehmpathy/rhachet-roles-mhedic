## .the etiology (a procedure can be a route)

- most often blunt/vehicular trauma; also urinary obstruction, **traumatic bladder expression or
  catheterization**, neoplasia, or post-surgical leakage.
  > formal, PMID 23470168 (Stafford & Bartges 2013, JVECC review): "Uroabdomen in dogs and cats is
  > **most often associated with vehicular or blunt trauma**. This condition may also result from
  > urinary tract obstruction, **traumatic bladder expression or catheterization**, neoplasia, and
  > postoperative leakage."

## .the worst-case trajectory (what powers the veto)

- a medical emergency: urine in the abdomen drives azotemia and hyperkalemia, with urosepsis and
  persistent azotemia among the complications.
  > formal, PMID 23470168: "**Uroabdomen is a medical emergency** … Potential complications include …
  > **urosepsis, unresolving azotemia** … or stricture formation"; diagnosis rests on abdominal
  > effusion with a fluid:serum creatinine ratio ≥ 2:1.

## .the best-case trajectory

- with a **dry tap / no bladder-wall breach**, no urine leaks and there is no uroabdomen; where a leak
  does occur, stabilization of fluids and hyperkalemia carries many cats through, and prognosis tracks
  the extent of injury.
  > formal, PMID 23470168: "**The prognosis … depends on the extent of urinary and nonurinary
  > injuries** as well as the development of complications"; acute management is fluid therapy +
  > hyperkalemia treatment (a medical, not primarily surgical, emergency).
