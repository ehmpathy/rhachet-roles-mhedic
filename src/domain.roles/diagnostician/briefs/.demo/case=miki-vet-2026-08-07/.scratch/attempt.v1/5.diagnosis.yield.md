## .the charge — the single most-plausible cause (a hypothesis, not a verdict)

**most-plausibly — until a hands-on exam confirms — acquired inflammatory pain of both hind limbs, a
vaccine-associated reaction** — i.e. injection-site myositis / soreness at the two hind-thigh vaccine
sites, or (if fever appears)
the systemic **FCV limping syndrome**. this is the **top hypothesis** from the `2.2` disentangle,
where the **vaccine** exposure ranked #1 (within a small, biased anecdote cohort — the forward
signal was **3 of 3 cases with a known outcome** (3 more of unknown outcome → a 50–100% range, an
upper bound, not a rate); clean, persistent, formally supported), **not** a verdict. restraint strain and the stacked-procedure
soreness are named **contributors**, not the charge.

> ⚠️ **this is a ranked hypothesis from biased anecdote + a small formal set, upper-bounded against
> the formal base rate (~52 vaccine-adverse-events / 10,000 cats — Moore 2007, PMID 17605670). it is NOT a confirmed diagnosis.**
> the confirm is a hands-on exam (see `3.assay.yield.md`); a licensed vet decides.

**confidence: low–moderate, provisional** (a small, biased cohort, lifted only by the formal support
and the drug-response pattern below). why this leads the lineup:

- both hind legs were instrumented the same day — a vaccine **and** a draw each side — but the
  **vaccine** arm is the one with a clean persistent signal and formal literature support; the draw
  arm's needle-attributable persistent-limp rate collapsed toward ~0 in the disentangle (`2.2.4`) —
  because every draw case there was mixed or transient (zero draw-*alone* persistent limps), while

- **persistence off-drug** (Sunday, the 2-day course finished): still frail and painful with no

- the anchor case is near-identical: a ~1yr FVRCP-FeLV cat with a post-vaccine hind-leg limp, screams

## .the medical facts, in full sun

- **vaccine-associated adverse events** in cats are dominated by **lethargy ± fever**, and
  **injection-site soreness is common** — Moore 2007 (PMID 17605670; ncbi-pmc, bhrowser-read).
- **FCV limping syndrome** is a documented post-vaccinal polyarthritis/lameness that can lag ~days —
  Balboni 2022 (PMID 35511315), Lanave 2023 (PMID 37889723; ncbi-pmc, bhrowser-read).
- **the Onsior (robenacoxib) dose is ~2.9× (≈3×) the label target.** owner-reported **12 mg/day** (2 × 6 mg)
  for a **9 lb / ~4.1 kg** cat. the **fda/dailymed** label states **1 mg/kg once daily** → target
  **~4 mg/day** for a 4.1 kg cat, so 12 mg/day is **~2.9 mg/kg ≈ 3× the label target**.
  **equal-weight caveat — this may be a dose-form artifact, not a chosen 3× dose:** the 6 mg tablet
  is the smallest single unit for a 2.5–6 kg cat, so any whole-tablet regimen already overshoots a
  4.1 kg cat's ~4 mg target, and a 2-tablet/day regimen lands ~3× it. the number is a fact to raise
  with the vet, **not evidence of an intentional overdose**. (bhrowser-verified; see
  `inventory.of=prescriptions.for=feline.case=robenacoxib-onsior.claims-official.safety.md` and
  `inventory.of=sources.case=trust.fda-dailymed`). **this is not a diagnosis of error** — a vet may
  have a rationale and this reads the label without her full chart. the **fact** stands on its own: an
  above-label NSAID dose raises the kidney / stomach / liver risk the label warns of, and **reduced
  appetite is one of the label's own stop-and-call signs** (the applied "surface this with the
  prescriber" step lives in *.the non-negotiable* below, not in this facts block).

## .the owner's questions, answered plainly

- **"was the blood draw the cause of the pain?"** — likely a **minor contributor** at most: the
  knee-area draw sites can be sore, but a limp that persists and worsens at day 2, plus a drop in
  appetite, points **beyond** a routine draw. in the disentangle the needle-attributable persistent
  limp collapsed toward ~0 (`2.2.4`) — that is, the rate of a persistent limp attributable to the
  needle itself fell to near-zero, not merely a gap in the data. it cannot be confirmed from history alone — and step one is to
  confirm **no bandage still constricts a leg** (intake reports the wraps were removed at home; the
  assay's urgent wrap gate re-confirms).
- **"is that why Onsior was prescribed?"** — **probably**, though only her vet can confirm the
  rationale. Onsior is an anti-inflammatory pain
  drug; a vet would reach for it to cover the expected pain from the stacked injections + draws. that
  she was comfortable while it was active shows it worked — then wore off. (see the ~3× dose flag — a
  fact about the label read without her full chart, **not a diagnosis of prescriber error**.)

## .the non-negotiable (acuity — holds wherever the cause ranks)

**reduced appetite + lethargy while on Onsior meets the drug's own label stop-and-call criterion** —
a documented sign on the fda/dailymed label — and she is now off-drug and still painful. against that

**pause / re-evaluate the Onsior**. **raise the ~2.9× dose at that same contact** — ask the vet
directly whether the above-label amount should change. escalation signs are in `4.triage.yield.md`.

## .what would raise or lower confidence

| observation | effect on the primary (vaccine-associated) read |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| a fever on rectal temperature | **raises** it — shifts toward systemic FCV limping syndrome |
| heat / a swell / a mass at a **vaccine** site | **raises** it — localizes to the injection |
| tenderness only at a **draw** site, none at vaccine sites | **lowers** it — shifts weight to the draw/contributor |
| a witnessed hard back-room hold / a pulled limb (assay 3b) | **raises** the restraint contributor |
| a leg cold or a bandage still tight | shifts to a venipuncture-wrap **emergency** |
| full ease + appetite back once Onsior is re-dosed to the label target | supports simple soreness (the #3 contributor) |
| a limp that localizes to one joint / bone on exam | opens an orthopedic path (a radiograph) |
