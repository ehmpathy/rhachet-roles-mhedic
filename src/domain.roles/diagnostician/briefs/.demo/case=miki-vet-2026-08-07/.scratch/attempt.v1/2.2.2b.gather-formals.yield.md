## .exposure 1 — vaccine → the strongest formal anchor

### the incidence + risk-factor study — a near-exact match to miki

**Moore GE, DeSantis-Kerr AC, Guptill LF, Glickman NW, Lewis HB, Glickman LT.**
*Adverse events after vaccine administration in cats: 2,560 cases (2002-2005).*
J Am Vet Med Assoc. 2007 Jul 1;231(1):94-100. **PMID: 17605670** (free article).
url: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17605670/

verbatim:

> "2,560 VAAEs were associated with administration of 1,258,712 doses of vaccine to 496,189
> cats (**51.6 VAAEs/10,000 cats vaccinated**). The **risk of a VAAE significantly increased as
> the number of vaccines administered per office visit increased**. **Risk was greatest for cats
> approximately 1 year old**; overall risk was greater for neutered versus sexually intact cats.
> **Lethargy with or without fever was the most commonly diagnosed VAAE.**"

> "young adult neutered cats that received **multiple vaccines per office visit** were at the
> greatest risk of a VAAE within 30 days after vaccination. Veterinarians should ... **limit the
> number of vaccinations administered concurrently to cats.**"

**why it matters for miki** — three of the study's named risk factors are exact matches:

| study risk factor | miki |
|-------------------|------|
| risk greatest at **~1 year old** | she is ~1 year (the 1-year wellness visit) |
| risk rises with **more vaccines per visit** | distemper-combo + rabies + felv in one visit |
| **lethargy ± fever** = most common VAAE | she showed lethargy + reduced appetite |

the base rate is low (~0.5%), but miki sits in the highest-risk cell the literature names.

### the mechanism — post-vaccinal "limping syndrome" (feline calicivirus)

the distemper-combination vaccine includes **feline calicivirus (FCV)**. FCV has a documented
**limping-syndrome pathotype**, seen both from natural infection and *from vaccination*.

**Balboni A, et al.** *Natural cases of polyarthritis associated with feline calicivirus
infection in cats.* Vet Res Commun. 2022 Jun;46(2):613-619. **PMID: 35511315 / PMC9165229**.
url: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35511315/

verbatim:

> "The limping syndrome is occasionally reported during acute feline calicivirus (FCV)
> infections or as consequence of vaccination."

**Lanave G, et al.** *An Outbreak of Limping Syndrome Associated with Feline Calicivirus.*
Animals (Basel). 2023 May 26;13(11):1778. **PMID: 37889723 / PMC10251824**.
url: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37889723/

verbatim:

> "Limping syndrome has been also described either in naturally or experimentally FCV-infected
> cats. In this study, we monitored a small outbreak of FCV infection in two household cats, in
> which limping disease was monitored with a 12-day lag time."

**Day MJ.** *Vaccine side effects: fact and fiction.* Vet Microbiol. 2006. **PMID: 16701964**
(review) — a range of suspected adverse reactions incl. inflammatory reactions.

**why it matters for miki** — post-vaccinal limping is a *real, named* entity, and it can lag
days (Lanave: 12-day lag), which fits a limp still present ~44 hours out. BUT the caveat below
keeps it from being a clean fit.

### ⚠️ the caveat that keeps FCV limping syndrome from being the obvious answer

classic FCV limping syndrome is typically **multi-limb / shifts across legs**, often **with
fever**, and usually resolves on its own. miki's picture is **hind-limb-localized** and
**dose-locked to onsior** — that pattern points more to *localized* injection-site / soft-tissue
soreness than to systemic FCV polyarthritis. the formal source proves the *category* exists; it
does not confirm *this* mechanism. a rectal temperature would help separate the two (fever nudges
toward FCV / systemic VAAE; no fever nudges toward local soreness).

## .exposure 2 — blood draw (venipuncture) → the informative *absence* of formal literature

pubmed search `venipuncture complications hematoma cat` returned **no cat-specific study of
post-venipuncture lameness**. the hits were tangential (fluid-therapy complications, thrombosis
in humans, jugular-draw bacteremia).

**the absence is itself a signal.** post-venipuncture soreness or a bruise in cats is a **minor,
transient** event — too benign to have generated a formal literature. that places it at the
*anecdote* level of evidence (exactly what the social gather would have captured), and it argues
*against* the blood draw as the driver of a lameness still worse at day 2. it does **not** rule
out a transient draw-site bruise as a *contributor*.

## .what the formal track settles, and what it cannot

| question | formal verdict |
|----------|----------------|
| is post-vaccinal lameness real in cats? | **yes** — named, documented (FCV limping syndrome; VAAEs) |
| does miki sit in a high-risk group? | **yes** — ~1yr + multiple concurrent vaccines + lethargy = the study's top-risk profile |
| can it lag ~2 days? | **yes** — documented lags up to ~12 days |
| is post-venipuncture lameness a formal entity? | **no** — absence of literature ⇒ minor/transient, anecdote-level |
| which single exposure caused *miki's* limp? | **cannot be settled from afar** — needs the exam + a temperature; social cohort still needed to weight the anecdote-level exposures |
