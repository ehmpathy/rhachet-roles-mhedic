## .the sunday signals (new — off all pain med)

with the onsior course finished, the drug's mask is gone — so what shows now is closer to the
true state. four new signals, each carried into the differential + triage:

1. **persistent pain off-drug.** she is still frail and painful **with no onsior on board**. a
   pain that outlasts a 2-day anti-inflammatory course is past routine post-procedure soreness —
   it favors a real lesion (the vaccine-site / soft-tissue arm) over a transient bruise.
2. **ungroomed hindquarters (tail, legs, thighs).** cats neglect to clean areas that hurt or
   cannot be reached. the neglect is **localized to the back half** — the exact region of both
   vaccine sites (both hind thighs) and both draw sites. a new, body-map-consistent pain sign.
3. **apparent constipation — tiny amounts.** possible contributors, not yet separated: reduced
   food/water intake, dehydration, pain on the squat-to-defecate posture, reduced mobility, or an
   nsaid gastrointestinal effect. flag for the vet; watch for strain-to-pass vs true obstipation.
4. **variable mobility.** she still made the counter at day's end, so mobility is **reduced, not
   lost** — consistent with pain-limited movement rather than a fixed mechanical block.

these strengthen track A (hind-limb pain, real lesion) and add a **constipation/hydration** watch
that `4.triage` must weigh. they do **not** create an emergency by themselves, but a persistent
off-drug pain + reduced defecation both push toward a **prompt re-exam**.

## .medications

- **onsior (robenacoxib)** — prescribed after the blood panel.
  - dose: **2 × 6 mg tablets = 12 mg, once per day, for 2 days** (owner-reported)
  - **⚠️ dose flag — raise with the prescriber promptly.** the bhrowser-verified fda/dailymed
    label states the dose is **1 mg/kg once daily**. at 9 lb (~4.1 kg) the label target is
    **~4 mg once daily** — roughly **one 6 mg tablet** (the 6 mg tablet is the standard single
    unit for a cat in the 2.5–6 kg band). **12 mg/day is about 3× the label target (~2.9 mg/kg).**
  - this is **not a diagnosis of error** — a veterinarian may have a rationale, and this reads
    the label from afar without her full chart. but it is a concrete discrepancy that a caller
    should confirm directly, because an above-label non-steroidal anti-inflammatory dose raises
    the kidney / stomach / liver risk the label warns of — and she is **now barely eating**, one
    of the label's own stop-and-call signs.
  - see: `inventory.of=prescriptions.for=feline.case=robenacoxib-onsior.claims-official.safety.md`

### quick answer — "what is chemistry"

a **blood chemistry panel**: a lab run on a blood sample that measures organ-function markers
— kidney values, liver values, blood sugar, proteins, electrolytes. it is the standard
baseline a veterinarian reviews before an anti-inflammatory drug like onsior, because that
drug leans on the kidneys and liver. (this is the "blood panel" the owner mentioned; the
pro b-type natriuretic peptide test rode along on the same draw as the heart-specific marker.)
