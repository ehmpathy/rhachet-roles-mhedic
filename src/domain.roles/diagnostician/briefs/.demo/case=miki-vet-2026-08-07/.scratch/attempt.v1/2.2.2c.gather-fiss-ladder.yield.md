## .the base rate — FISS is rare

**Merck Veterinary Manual** — *Connective Tissue Tumors in Animals*.
url: https://www.merckvetmanual.com/integumentary-system/tumors-of-the-skin-and-soft-tissues/connective-tissue-tumors-in-animals

> "These tumors develop in some cats (**1–16 cases/10,000 vaccinations**) due to chronic
> inflammation following administration of adjuvanted vaccines, inactivated rabies vaccines,
> long-acting corticosteroid injections, or other injections."

→ 0.01%–0.16%. (Catster, vet-reviewed, framed lifetime risk as "less than 0.1%".)

## .the treatment ladder (in order of escalation)

### rung 0 — detect + biopsy (the "3-2-1" trigger)

**Today's Veterinary Practice** — *Feline Injection Site Sarcomas: Risk Factors, Diagnosis,
Staging, and Treatment Algorithm.*
url: https://todaysveterinarypractice.com/oncology/feline-injection-site-sarcomas

> "The VAFSTF recommends the '3-2-1' rule for when to pursue biopsy of a suspect injection site
> sarcoma. If a mass meets one or more of the following criteria, an incisional biopsy is
> recommended:" — **3**: mass persists **> 3 months** after injection; **2**: mass is/becomes
> **larger than 2 cm**; **1**: mass **continues to increase in size 1 month** following an
> injection.

> "Owners should contact their veterinarian if they note a lump, especially one increasing in
> size or persisting beyond 1 month after vaccination."

**VCA** — *Post-Vaccination Sarcoma in Cats.*
url: https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/post-vaccination-sarcoma-in-cats

> "A biopsy of the lump and histopathology (microscopic examination) of the abnormal tissue will
> confirm whether it is a sarcoma, as opposed to inflammation or infection."

> ⚠️ TVP caveat — do not just watch a confirmed-suspect: "Cats with suspected FISS should not be
> monitored—referral to a veterinary oncologist or surgeon is encouraged." (the "3-2-1" rule is
> the trigger to *act*, not a license to wait past it.)

### rung 1 — radical surgery (first-line)

**Today's Veterinary Practice** (same url):

> "first-line therapy for FISSs is aggressive radical surgery."

> "The best outcomes are obtained when an **initial radical surgical excision** is performed by a
> surgical specialist."

recommended margins (from the surgical literature surfaced in search — sagepub 2019):
"5 cm lateral margins and two fascial planes for deep margins."

### rung 1b — amputation (for a limb FISS)

**VCA** (same url):

> "The tumor, as well as a generous amount of tissue around its margins, must be surgically
> removed to reduce the likelihood of recurrence. Depending on the size and position of the
> tumor, **amputation of a limb** or other tissues may be necessary."

### rung 2 — radiation (adjuvant, pre- or post-op)

**NC State Veterinary Hospital** — *Medical Oncology: Feline Injection Site Sarcoma.*
url: https://hospital.cvm.ncsu.edu/services/small-animals/cancer-oncology/oncology/feline-injection-site-sarcoma/

> "Radiation therapy can be done prior to, or after, surgical removal of the tumor… Radiation
> therapy combined with surgery has been shown to significantly improve survival over surgery
> alone."

### rung 3 — chemotherapy (high-grade / metastatic / unresectable)

**NC State** (same url):

> "Chemotherapy is recommended as part of a treatment plan for most injection site sarcomas.
> Spread to distant sites in the body occurs in up to 25% of cats."

**Today's Veterinary Practice** (palliative, macroscopic disease):

> "Cats with macroscopic tumors that cannot be resected… can be treated palliatively with
> ifosfamide (~3% complete response, 37% partial response), doxorubicin combined with
> cyclophosphamide (50% partial response), or lomustine… Responses to these drugs are brief
> (median, 2.5 to 4 months)."

### rung 3b — newer adjuncts

NC State: "Alternative options include immunotherapy with an IL-2 vaccine and electrochemotherapy."

## .how likely is amputation? — the honest picture

there is **no single published "% amputated"** figure; it is **location-dependent**. the logic:

- leg placement exists precisely to **make amputation a clean cure option**:
  **AAHA** (2020 guidelines): "the Task Force recommends vaccination in the lower distal limbs to
  facilitate clean margins **if surgical amputation is required**."
  **TVP**: distal-limb placement is "To facilitate earlier detection and increase the likelihood of
  complete resection **with amputation of the affected limb**."
- so for a **limb** FISS, amputation is frequently the treatment of choice — and, counterintuitively,
  a limb tumor carries a **better** prognosis than an interscapular one (which invades the spine and
  cannot be cleanly excised).
- the one caveat that defeats amputation — **TVP**: "Cats can develop tumors along more than 1 limb…
  making amputation an ineffective option for such patients."

## .the prognosis / survival distribution

| scenario | outcome | source |
|----------|---------|--------|
| overall local recurrence | "recurrence rates approach **70%**, especially in the absence of radical surgical procedures" | TVP |
| metastasis (distant spread) | "metastatic rate… ranges from **0–25%**" / "up to 25% of cats" | Upstate / NC State |
| wide excision (4–5 cm margins) | "median survival is **800 days or more**" (~2.2+ yrs) | Upstate Vet Specialties |
| less-aggressive margins (2–3 cm) | "median survival is around **400 days**" (~1.1 yr) | Upstate |
| radical vs marginal *first* excision | median time to first recurrence **325 days vs 79 days** | VIN / WSAVA 2017 (search-surfaced) |
| surgery + radiation | "significantly improve survival over surgery alone" | NC State |
| after recurrence + local failure | "humane euthanasia within **2 to 3 months** of recurrence is the most common cause of death" | TVP |

**Upstate Vet Specialties** — *Specialty Spotlight: Feline Injection Site Sarcomas.*
url: https://www.uvsonline.com/specialty-spotlight-feline-injection-site-sarcomas/

> "Despite the high risk for local recurrence, the overall prognosis for cats with FISS is good.
> When treated with wide surgical excision (4-5cm), the median survival is 800 days or more. When
> treated with less aggressive surgical margins (2-3cm), the median survival is around 400 days.
> Prognostic factors include tumor size, treatment type, mitotic count, local recurrence, and the
> presence of metastatic disease. The metastatic rate of FISS ranges from 0-25%."

**Today's Veterinary Practice** (recurrence + terminal course):

> "recurrence rates approach 70%, especially in the absence of radical surgical procedures."
> "local treatment failure leading to humane euthanasia within 2 to 3 months of recurrence is the
> most common cause of death in cats with FISS."

## .the distribution in plain terms

- **most cats never get FISS** (~0.01–0.16% per vaccination).
- **if** caught early, on a limb, and treated with radical first surgery + clean margins →
  **median survival 2+ years**, and amputation can be **curative**.
- the **bad tail** is recurrence (~70% without radical surgery) → survival compresses toward ~1
  year; once it recurs and local control fails, decline is fast (**2–3 months**).
- **early + aggressive + first-surgery-done-right dominates the whole distribution.**
