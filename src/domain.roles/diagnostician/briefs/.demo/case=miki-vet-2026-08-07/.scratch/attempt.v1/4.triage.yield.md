## .acuity: elevated — same-day vet contact, not watchful-wait

four facts together lift this above "just sore":

1. **reduced appetite + lethargy while on onsior** — the drug's own label says **stop and call**
   in exactly this case (bhrowser-verified, prescriber briefs).
2. **the lameness has not improved at ~44 hours** — routine post-procedure soreness usually
   eases by day 2.
3. **reduced appetite in a cat is risky on its own** — a cat that eats too little for too long
   can develop a serious liver problem.
4. **a new, dose-locked functional deficit** — off onsior she **tries and cannot** hop more than
   ~5 inches (a loss that began only after the visit, not her baseline); on onsior she jumps onto
   the counter. an acquired "cannot", reversed by an anti-inflammatory, is a real lesion signal —
   not ordinary soreness that would ride out untreated.

it is saturday. the responsible move is to **contact the veterinarian who prescribed the
onsior, or an emergency animal hospital, today** — report the appetite drop and the persistent
limp, and ask whether to **pause the onsior** until she is seen.

## .escalate to emergency-now if any of these appear

> ⚠️ **if this is a medical emergency, call your local emergency number or an emergency animal
> hospital now.** the list below is a **watch-for**, not a description of her current state — none
> of these are present as of sat 2026-08-08, 1:30pm. any one appearing flips this to emergency-now.

- will not eat at all for more than ~24 hours
- will not stand or will not bear weight on a leg
- a leg is cold, swollen, or a bandage is still on and tight → **do not wait**
- repeat vomit episodes, or dark / tarry stool
- pale or yellow gums
- collapse, labored breath, or a seizure

(these mirror the onsior label's serious-reaction signs — kidney, liver, gastrointestinal, or
neurologic — which the label notes can appear abruptly and, in rare cases, prove fatal.)
