# 2.1.differential.enumerate

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** these are candidate causes to investigate,
> **hypotheses, not asserted facts**. a licensed veterinarian who examines the patient decides.
> drug-label claims below trace to the bhrowser-verified onsior briefs; the clinical read is
> provisional and must be confirmed. see `rule.require.bhrowser-citations`.

the picture: a young cat, ~44 hours after a visit that stacked **many** procedures on **both
hind legs** — vaccines, repeat venipuncture, a cystocentesis attempt — now with a hind-limb
lameness, retreat under the bed, and reduced appetite. the lameness eased fully while onsior
was active and returned when it wore off.

## .ranked candidate causes

### 1. post-procedure soreness / inflammation of both hind limbs — *most likely*

both hind legs took an injection **and** a blood draw. vaccines go into muscle or under the
skin; the knee-area bandages fit the medial saphenous vein, a common feline draw site near the
knee. multi-site soft-tissue soreness fits a young, otherwise-well cat.

- **supports:** it eased completely on onsior (an anti-inflammatory) and returned when the dose
  wore off — a hallmark of inflammatory / soft-tissue pain, not a fixed structural injury.
- **against / watch:** routine soreness usually *improves* by day 2. a limp that persists or
  worsens at ~44 hours, plus reduced appetite, is past "just sore" and earns a re-exam.

### 2. vaccine-associated local reaction

transient local inflammation and soreness at a vaccine site, sometimes with mild lethargy,
reduced appetite, and a low-grade fever 1–2 days after. common and usually self-limited.

- **supports:** the time course and the sore hind thighs.
- **assay:** a rectal temperature; a vet's palpation of each site for heat, a swell, or a mass.

### 3. venipuncture-site bruise / hematoma or discomfort (knee-area draw sites)

a draw can leave a small bruise or tenderness. usually mild; resolves on its own.

- **watch — urgent sub-case:** a bandage left on too long can constrict a limb. if any wrap is
  still on and the foot is cold or swollen, that is an emergency, not a wait.

### 4. pain / strain from the restraint event

the ~5 minutes of screams and moans may reflect a painful or forceful restraint; a strain or a
tweak is possible.

### 5. lower-likelihood, keep on the radar

- a more significant injection reaction, or discomfort tied to the cystocentesis attempt
- an unrelated orthopedic issue that the visit merely unmasked

## .separate track — do not conflate

the **pant episodes** that prompted the pro b-type natriuretic peptide test are a **cardiac
question**, a different investigation from the hind-limb lameness. keep the two apart; the
heart marker result feeds its own path.

## .the owner's questions, addressed

- **"was the blood draw harmful — the cause of the pain?"** — cannot be judged from afar.
  hind-leg draws can leave transient soreness or a bruise, so the draw sites are a plausible
  *contributor*. but a lameness that worsens at day 2 with reduced appetite points past a
  routine draw — a hands-on re-exam is the honest next step. first, confirm no bandage still
  constricts a leg.
- **"is that why onsior was prescribed?"** — most likely **yes**. onsior is an
  anti-inflammatory pain drug; a veterinarian would reach for it to cover the expected
  post-procedure pain from the stacked injections and draws. that she was comfortable while it
  was active is consistent with inflammatory pain — the drug did its job, then wore off.

## .the decisive flag

reduced appetite + lethargy in a cat **on onsior** meets the drug's own label line to
**stop and call** — *"Stop administration of ONSIOR if appetite decreases or if the cat becomes
lethargic"* (bhrowser-verified, prescriber briefs). that alone lifts this out of watchful-wait.
carried into `4.triage.yield.md`.
