# 3.assay

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** this names WHICH checks would confirm or
> rule out each ranked cause — a **hypothesis-testing plan** for a licensed veterinarian to run,
> not a self-test list. a vet who examines miki decides. the referrer decides WHERE. see
> `define.diagnostician-scope`, `rule.require.recommendation-disclaimer`.

the assay for the re-ranked candidates from `2.2.5.attribute-rerank`
(**#1 vaccine reaction ▸ #2 restraint strain ▸ #3 combined soreness ▸ #4 venipuncture bruise ▸
#5 lower-likelihood**). each check names the test, what it tells us, and which candidate it
targets — sequenced most-urgent / cheapest first.

## .boundary

the diagnostician **names** the checks. a licensed veterinarian **performs** them; the
**referrer** decides where; the moment it turns to treatment — pause the drug, switch it, add pain
cover — that is the **prescriber's** work. none below is an at-home procedure.

## .the sequence

### 0. URGENT gate — rule out a constricting bandage (do first, zero cost)

- **check:** is any wrap still on a hind leg? is the foot below it cold, swollen, or discolored?
- **tells us:** a wrap left on too long can constrict a limb — an emergency, not a candidate to
  rank. this gates everything else.
- **targets:** the urgent sub-case of #4 (venipuncture-site wrap).
- **if yes → emergency re-exam now**, ahead of all below.

### 0b. the decisive action — already triggered, carry it in

two owner-observed signals already force a re-exam now — neither is a test to sequence; together
they are the **reason a re-exam happens now** and the container the checks below run inside, and
they override the rank regardless of which cause leads:

- **the stop-and-call flag:** reduced appetite + lethargy **on onsior** meets the drug's own
  **stop-and-call** line (bhrowser-verified prescriber briefs).
- **the dose-locked functional deficit:** **off** onsior she **tries and cannot** hop more than
  ~5 inches (a *new* post-visit loss, not her baseline); **on** onsior she jumps onto the counter
  and eats. a near-complete, dose-locked response on an anti-inflammatory is itself strong evidence
  of **acquired inflammatory hind-limb pain** — the owner has, in effect, already run an informal
  drug-response assay, and its result corroborates the #1/#2 inflammatory-lesion reads.

### 1. rectal temperature (cheap, fast, high-separation)

- **check:** a rectal temperature.
- **tells us:** fever points to a **systemic** process — the FCV limping-syndrome sub-read or a
  broader vaccine reaction; a normal temperature favors a **local** injection-site soreness.
- **targets:** #1 vaccine reaction — splits its two sub-reads (local myositis vs systemic FCV
  limping syndrome). first, because it is the single cheapest test that splits the lead candidate.

### 2. palpation of each injection + draw site (cheap, localizes)

- **check:** a vet palpates each vaccine site (both hind thighs) and each knee-area draw site for
  heat, swelling, a discrete mass, or a pain response.
- **tells us:** heat/swelling at a **vaccine** site supports #1; tenderness only at a **draw**
  site supports #4; pain at neither, spread through the limb, favors #2/#3.
- **targets:** #1 (vaccine), #4 (venipuncture bruise), #3 (diffuse soreness, by exclusion).

### 3. gait + limb exam with joint range-of-motion (cheap, localizes the lesion)

- **check:** a vet watches the gait and flexes/extends each hind joint, notes pain on motion,
  instability, or a focal muscle tender-point.
- **tells us:** a focal muscle tender-point / single-joint pain supports a **strain** (#2
  restraint); pain across **multiple** joints supports FCV polyarthritis (#1 systemic); a prior
  joint change hints at an orthopedic issue the visit unmasked (#5).
- **targets:** #2 restraint strain, #1 (FCV polyarthritis sub-read), #5 (unmasked orthopedic).

### 3b. history probe — the back-room restraint (closes a data-gap, zero cost)

- **check:** ask the owner + the clinic what the ~5-minute back-room hold looked like — was she
  scruffed, stretched, or forcibly held? was a limb pulled? the ~5 min of screams is a distress
  proxy, but the hold itself was **unwitnessed** by the owner.
- **tells us:** the disentangle capped restraint at a **co-contributor** precisely because the
  hold is an *unobserved* exposure (an unfalsifiable absence). a direct account converts that gap
  into evidence — a witnessed hard hold or a pulled limb would *raise* the restraint read; a gentle
  hold would lower it.
- **targets:** #2 restraint strain — the one check that can move its rank on real data, not
  inference.

### 4. review the records — chemistry, proBNP, onsior dose vs weight

- **check:** pull the blood chemistry + pro b-type natriuretic peptide (proBNP) results; confirm
  the onsior dose, schedule, day count, and the patient's weight against the label floor.
- **tells us:** baseline organ function; the heart-marker answer (separate track); whether the
  onsior dose fits this cat — a **data point** for the prescriber, not a pause decision made here.
- **targets:** context for all; feeds the separate cardiac track and the prescriber.

### 5. (conditional) a radiograph of the painful limb

- **when:** only if the limp **localizes and persists** after steps 1–3.
- **tells us:** rules out a fracture or a joint lesion.
- **targets:** #5 (unmasked orthopedic), the structural tail of #2.

### 6. (conditional) imaging or fine-needle aspirate of a persistent site swelling

- **when:** only if step 2 finds a **discrete swelling that persists or grows**.
- **tells us:** separates a benign transient bruise from a sterile injection-site abscess or a
  more significant injection reaction.
- **targets:** the severe-reaction tail of #1.

## .separate track — do not conflate

the **pant episodes** that prompted the proBNP test are a **cardiac** question — a different
investigation. its result feeds its own path; keep it apart from the hind-limb assay above.

## .done-when check

each top candidate has a confirm-or-rule-out check, urgent-first:

| candidate | primary check(s) |
|-----------|------------------|
| #1 vaccine reaction | temperature (1) + site palpation (2) [+ 6 conditional] |
| #2 restraint strain | gait + joint exam (3) + back-room-hold history probe (3b) [+ 5 conditional] |
| #3 combined soreness | site palpation (2), by exclusion |
| #4 venipuncture bruise | urgent wrap gate (0) + site palpation (2) |
| #5 lower-likelihood | joint exam (3) + radiograph (5) |
