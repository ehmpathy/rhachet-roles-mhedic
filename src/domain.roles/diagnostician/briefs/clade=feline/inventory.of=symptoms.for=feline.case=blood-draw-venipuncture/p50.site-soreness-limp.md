# p50.site-soreness-limp

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .symptom

a sore leg or mild limp at the draw site — favors the leg, careful sits, less jump. **bilateral if
both legs were drawn** (e.g. a blown vein on one leg forces a second stick on the other), so a
sore-both-back-legs pattern can follow a two-site draw.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p50** (common — a routine, mild reaction; an ordinal bucket, not a measured rate)
- **onset:** same day–hours after the draw
- **duration:** **~1–2 days** (typically self-limited)

## .anchor

From [Dial A Vet — cat limping after a blood draw](https://www.dialavet.com/vet-answers/post/cat-limping-after-blood-draw-23205):
> "Your cat's limping after a blood draw is likely a temporary reaction. This occurs occasionally
> and tends to resolve without further issues." / "Typically, any limping will resolve within 24 to
> 48 hours."

## .crosses into a hazard when

the limp is severe, the cat **won't bear weight at all**, or it persists/worsens past ~2 days → see
the companion `../inventory.of=hazards.for=feline.case=blood-draw-venipuncture.md`.
