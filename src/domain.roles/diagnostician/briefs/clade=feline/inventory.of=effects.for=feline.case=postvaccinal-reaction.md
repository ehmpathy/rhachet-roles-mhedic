# inventory.of=effects.for=feline.case=postvaccinal-reaction

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** this brief is research groundwork compiled
> from public sources to the best of our knowledge; it carries **no guarantee of outcome** and is
> **not a substitute for a licensed veterinarian** who has examined your cat and knows its full
> history. if your cat collapses, has trouble breathing, has facial swelling, or vomits/has
> diarrhea repeatedly after a vaccine, that can be an **emergency — contact a veterinarian or an
> emergency veterinary service now.**

## .what

after a routine vaccine, most cats feel the effects **for about 24–48 hours, occasionally up to a
few days.** the common signs are **mild and self-limited**: soreness at the injected leg (a limp,
reluctance to jump, slow to sit), lower energy (more sleep, less play), a reduced appetite, and
sometimes a mild fever. these are the expected consequences of the immune system doing its job,
not a sign the vaccine "gave" the cat a disease.

this brief traces that reaction through the diagnostician's **four-node causal chain** (see
`../define.causal-chain-frame.[lesson].md`) so the exposure (vaccination) can be attributed to the
symptoms observed, and separated from the rare serious reactions that need a vet.

## .the worked case — two hind-leg injections

the case that motivated this brief: a cat given **feline leukemia (FeLV) vaccine in the left
thigh**, and **FVRCP ("feline distemper" — panleukopenia + herpesvirus + calicivirus) plus rabies
in the right thigh.**

- **both hind limbs were injected**, so **bilateral hind-leg soreness** is exactly what the
  exposure predicts — a limp, no jumps, slow/careful sits, favoring the back legs.
- distal-limb (leg) placement is the **deliberate modern convention**, not a mistake. from the
  [ABCD/Hartmann FISS guideline](https://www.abcdcatsvets.org/guideline-for-feline-injection-site-sarcoma/):
  > "Vaccines should be injected at sites where any subsequent mass could be easily surgically removed, preferably distally in a limb."
- FeLV and rabies are the **two vaccines historically most associated** with injection-site
  reactions and, rarely, sarcoma — bhrowser verified 2026-08-10.

## .the causal chain

```
cause:exposure ──▶ cause:mechanism ──▶ effect:mechanism ──▶ effect:symptom
```

| node | in this case |
|------|--------------|
| **cause:exposure** | the 3 antigen injections (FeLV left thigh; FVRCP + rabies right thigh) |
| **cause:mechanism** | needle/tissue trauma + a deliberate **adjuvant-driven local inflammatory reaction** + systemic immune (adaptive + innate acute-phase) activation |
| **effect:mechanism** | local injection-site inflammation / mild myositis in each injected limb; a transient whole-body acute-phase state |
| **effect:symptom** | leg soreness → limp / no jumps / slow sits; tiredness → more sleep, less play; reduced appetite; sometimes mild fever |

## .how long — the duration table

| effect | typical duration | source signal |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| lower energy, reduced appetite, mild fever | **24–48 h**, occasionally a few days | VCA, PDSA, AVMA, iCatCare |
| injection-site soreness / limp / reluctance to jump | ~**1–3 days** | AVMA, iCatCare |
| small firm lump under the skin at the site | can linger **~1–2 weeks** (usually benign) | AVMA |
| **anaphylaxis** (collapse, facial swelling, hard breathing, repeated vomit) | **minutes–hours** → emergency | AVMA, VCA |
| **feline injection-site sarcoma (FISS)** — rare | **months–years** later | ABCD, AVMA, Cornell |

## .why — the mechanism behind each symptom

the symptoms are the **intended immune reaction**, not an infection. two levers drive them:

**1. local inflammation at the injected leg → soreness, limp, a small lump.**
killed/inactivated vaccines (rabies, many FeLV products) carry an **adjuvant** whose whole job is
to provoke local inflammation so the immune system pays attention. From
[VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/vaccines-for-cats):
> "killed vaccines may have an adjuvant (added ingredient) to make the immune response stronger."

From the [ABCD/Hartmann guideline](https://www.abcdcatsvets.org/guideline-for-feline-injection-site-sarcoma/):
> "Adjuvants are mostly added to enhance a pro-inflammatory reaction at the site of injection, which is intended and a necessity when applying a killed agent in order to trigger the required immune response."

that deliberate inflammation is what makes the leg tender — hence the limp, the reluctance to
jump, the careful sit. FISS-relevant note: the hindlimb is a recognized injection/reaction site —
> "FISS occur at sites typically used for vaccination and injections, such as the interscapular region ... and in the area of the semimembranosus and semitendinosus muscles in the hindlimbs."

**2. systemic immune activation → tiredness, more sleep, less play, reduced appetite, mild fever.**
the same immune cascade that trains protection also produces a transient whole-body "acute-phase"
malaise — the cat feels run-down for a day or two, the same way a person can after a flu shot.
Cornell frames the intended action plainly ([Feline Vaccines: Benefits and Risks](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-vaccines-benefits-and-risks)):
> "After vaccination, the immune system is 'trained' to recognize infectious agents by producing proteins called antibodies or activating specific cells to kill the agents."

## .attribution — the two symptom patterns you asked about

both patterns are **classic, expected post-vaccinal effects** — and here the timing (right after
the shots) and the site (both hind legs) make the vaccine the **leading explanation**:

| observed symptom | fits vaccine exposure? | why |
|------------------|------------------------|-----|
| **clear pain in the back legs** — no jumps, slow sits, limps | **highly consistent** | both thighs were injected; local adjuvant inflammation makes each leg sore. bilateral because both legs got shots. expected to ease within ~1–3 days |
| **tiredness, more sleep, less play** | **highly consistent** | the systemic acute-phase reaction; expected to ease within 24–48 h |

**caveat (the honest part of attribution):** "consistent with" is not "certainly caused by." the
causal-chain frame keeps other exposures on the table — e.g. rough restraint at the visit, a prior
sore joint, or an unrelated injury. what tips the read toward *vaccine* here is the **tight
timing** and the **bilateral hind-leg pattern matching the two injection sites.** the strongest
way to be sure is **time**: a true vaccine reaction fades on the schedule above. pain that is
**severe, worsening, or still there after 2–3 days** breaks the expected pattern and warrants a
vet.

## .red flags — when it is NOT just a normal reaction

**call a vet now (possible anaphylaxis — minutes to hours after the shot).** From
[AVMA](https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/vaccinations):
> "Fainting or collapse / Persistent vomiting or diarrhea / Persistent and severe coughing / Difficulty breathing / Red, itchy bumps all over the body ('hives') / Swelling or puffiness in or around the face, nose, eyes, or neck"

**call a vet soon if the mild signs overstay:** lethargy/soreness/appetite loss lasting **more
than ~2 days**, or the cat seems **extremely uncomfortable**.

**watch the lump — the "3-2-1 rule" for FISS.** a small firm lump can be a benign reaction that
fades in a couple weeks, but a persistent one needs checking. From the
[ABCD/Hartmann guideline](https://www.abcdcatsvets.org/guideline-for-feline-injection-site-sarcoma/):
> "Any lump at the site of injection that is still present three months after vaccination, or that is larger than 2 cm in diameter, or that is increasing in size one month after vaccination should be surgically removed and investigated through histopathology."

FISS is **rare** — from [iCatCare](https://icatcare.org/articles/vaccinating-your-cat):
> "In very rare cases (around less than 1 in 20,000 vaccines given), a feline injection site sarcoma (FISS) could develop".

and from the [ABCD guideline](https://www.abcdcatsvets.org/guideline-for-feline-injection-site-sarcoma/), inflammatory reactions are far more common than sarcoma:
> "the reported rates were 0.3 cases of FISS per 10,000 vaccines and 11.8 post-vaccinal inflammatory reactions per 10,000 vaccinations in cats".

## .per-vaccine notes for this case

- **FeLV (left thigh)** — a retrovirus vaccine, core for kittens. From
  [Cornell — Feline Leukemia Virus](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-leukemia-virus):
  > "This vaccine is also now considered a core vaccine for kittens, due to their higher risk of developing progressive infection."
  reassurance for later testing: > "FeLV vaccines will not cause false positive FeLV results on ELISA, IFA, or any other available FeLV tests."
- **FVRCP (right thigh, "feline distemper" combo)** — panleukopenia + herpesvirus + calicivirus;
  the **calicivirus** component is worth knowing because calicivirus itself can cause a transient
  lameness, so a limp here can be either the injection soreness or a mild vaccine-strain effect.
- **rabies (right thigh)** — killed/adjuvanted historically; one of the two vaccines (with FeLV)
  first linked to injection-site reactions, which is why distal-limb placement is used.

## .bottom line

- **duration:** expect the tiredness/appetite dip to ease in **24–48 h**, and the sore-leg limp in
  about **1–3 days.**
- **the back-leg pain and the tiredness are both textbook post-vaccinal effects** here — the
  timing and the two hind-leg sites line up.
- **escalate** if there are anaphylaxis signs (now), if mild signs last **> 2–3 days** or the cat
  is in obvious pain, or if a lump at a site **persists/grows** (the 3-2-1 rule).
- none of this replaces a veterinarian who can examine the cat.

## .scientific evidence (peer-reviewed)

the client-education picture above is corroborated by the primary literature. all abstracts read
through the bhrowser (Europe PMC) on 2026-08-10, quoted verbatim.

### the reaction is an immune reaction — its four mechanisms

From [Hartmann et al. 2023, *Viruses* — VAAEs in cats, PMC10459272](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37632050):
> "VAAEs are caused by an aberrant innate or adaptive immune reaction, excessive local reactions at the inoculation site, an error in administration, or failure in the manufacturing process."

> "No injectable vaccine is risk-free, and therefore, vaccination should be performed as often as necessary, but as infrequently as possible."

### why FISS forms — chronic inflammation at the injection site

From [Hartmann et al. 2015, *J Feline Med Surg* — ABCD guideline, PMC11148925](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26101312):
> "chronic inflammatory reactions are considered the trigger for subsequent malignant transformation. ... Adjuvanted vaccines induce intense local inflammation and seem therefore to be particularly linked to the development of FISS. The risk is lower for modified-live and recombinant vaccines, but no vaccine is risk-free."

> "The rate of metastasis ranges from 10-28%."

From [Lu et al. 2023, *J Cell Mol Med*, PMC10399534](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37334757):
> "there is a consensus that FISS is associated with chronic inflammation caused by irritation of injection-related trauma and foreign chemical substances."

From [Shih et al. 2022, *BMC Vet Res*, PMC9281114](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35836213):
> "The pathogenesis of FISS has been correlated with chronic inflammation, resulting in neoplastic transformation."

From [Santelices Iglesias et al. 2018, *J Comp Pathol*](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30502795):
> "Vaccine adjuvants can trigger an intense and persistent inflammatory response that may lead to neoplastic transformation."

From [Hsueh et al. 2019, *BMC Vet Res*, PMC6815016](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31653220):
> "The disease has been considered to be associated with vaccine adjuvant, aluminum, which serves as a stimulus continuously inducing overzealous inflammatory and immunologic reactions."

From [Wei et al. 2019, *BMC Cancer*, PMC6449919](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30947707):
> "Feline injection-site sarcoma (FISS), an aggressive iatrogenic subcutaneous malignancy, is challenging to manage clinically".

### how common — incidence figures

From [Kliczkowska et al. 2015, *Pol J Vet Sci*](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26172181):
> "The prevalence is estimated between 1 in 1000 and 1 in 10 000 vaccinations in the United Kingdom."

> "the prevalence of FISS was 0.16% (16 FISS on 10.000 of cats) ... general practice and 85 on 10.000 cats in a practice focused on veterinary oncology."

### the scientific uncertainty (kept honest)

From [Kass 2017, *Vet Clin North Am Small Anim Pract*](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29217316):
> "It is scientifically untenable to address FISS risk based on propensity of vaccines to elicit differential inflammatory responses if none of those responses led to sarcoma development. ... such guidelines must be regarded with skepticism."

### vaccine type, adjuvant, and post-vaccinal fever

From [Aida et al. 2021, *Front Vet Sci*, PMC8083957](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33937377):
> "The efficacy of inactivated vaccines can be suboptimal with particular pathogens and safety concerns arise with live-attenuated vaccines."

From [Stachura et al. 2025, *Vaccines* — non-adjuvanted rabies, PMC12737566](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41441643):
> "Although a number of vaccines exist for domestic animals, most contain inactivated rabies virus with adjuvants."

> "young kittens and puppies can be safely vaccinated without serious adverse effects."

From [Sinha et al. 2025, *Vaccines*, PMC12656953](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41295545) — a concrete post-vaccinal fever, seen 24 h out:
> "100 μg pan-CoV vaccine-1 caused fever 24 h later, which was resolved by a single Meloxicam treatment."

From [Almeras et al. 2017, *BMC Vet Res*, PMC5635571](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29017551) — an FeLV/RCP vaccine trial:
> "the efficacy of two vaccines (Leucofeligen™ FeLV/RCP and Purevax™ RCP FeLV), which differ by their nature (live vs. inactivated) and the vaccinal strains".

### the FVRCP components and the schedule

From [Wang et al. 2025, *Sci Rep* — 4,736 cats, PMC12804871](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41372388):
> "Feline panleukopenia virus (FPV), feline calicivirus (FCV), and feline herpesvirus-1 (FHV-1) substantially impact feline health. ... These results support a two-dose primary series for cats aged ≥ 3 months, followed by periodic boosters".

From [Scherk et al. 2013 — AAFP Feline Vaccination Advisory Panel Report, *J Feline Med Surg*, PMC11110975](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23966005):
> "This Report was developed by the Feline Vaccination Advisory Panel of the American Association of Feline Practitioners (AAFP) to provide practical recommendations to help clinicians select appropriate vaccination schedules for their feline patients based on risk assessment."

From [RSPCA — Vaccinating cats and kittens](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/cats/health/vaccinations):
> "Your kitten will need two sets of vaccinations to get them started - their first set at nine weeks old and a second booster set at three months old. After this, kittens and cats usually need 'booster' vaccinations once a year."
