# p20.intraluminal-obstruction

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .cause

**intraluminal obstruction** — firm, poorly-digested matter (hair, bone, litter) mixed into feces
that cannot pass; a colonic stricture can do the same from within.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p20** (a real, frequent contributor; Merck calls this type "most common" among obstructive causes; ordinal bucket)
- **causal-chain:** `ingested indigestible matter (fur/bone/litter) → firm mass can't pass → impaction → retained feces dry further`

## .anchor

From [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-large-intestine-in-small-animals/constipation-obstipation-and-megacolon-in-small-animals):
> "Intraluminal obstruction is most common. Intraluminal obstruction develops due to the inability to pass poorly digested, often firm matter (eg, hair, bones, litter) mixed with fecal material and is secondary to a lack of sufficient water intake ... or reluctance to defecate. A colonic stricture could also result in intraluminal obstruction."

From [Cornell Feline Health Center — Constipation](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/constipation):
> "ingestion of indigestible material (i.e. fur), obstruction of the GI tract by foreign bodies, tumors, or strictures (narrowings) ... can predispose to constipation in cats."

## .note

long-haired cats and heavy groomers ingest more fur → more likely to form the impacting matrix.
grooming and hairball diets are cited mitigations.
