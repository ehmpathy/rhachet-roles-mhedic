# p10.neurologic-spinal

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .cause

**neurologic / spinal lesion** — loss of the nerve supply to the colon and rectum, e.g. the Manx
sacral spinal-cord deformity or pelvic-nerve injury.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p10** (uncommon; breed- or injury-specific; ordinal bucket)
- **causal-chain:** `spinal / pelvic-nerve lesion → lost colorectal innervation → no propulsive/defecation reflex → feces retained`

## .anchor

From [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-large-intestine-in-small-animals/constipation-obstipation-and-megacolon-in-small-animals):
> "lesions of the spinal cord (eg, Manx sacral spinal cord deformity) or pelvic nerves".

From [Cornell Feline Health Center — Constipation](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/constipation):
> "neuromuscular disease ... can predispose to constipation in cats."

## .tell

a **Manx** (tailless) cat, or a cat with hindlimb/tail neuro signs or incontinence, raises this.
distinct from megacolon in that the primary lesion is the *nerve*, not the colon wall.
