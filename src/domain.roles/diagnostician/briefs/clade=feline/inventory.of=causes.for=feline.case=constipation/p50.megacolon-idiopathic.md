# p50.megacolon-idiopathic

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .cause

**idiopathic megacolon** — a dilated, hypomotile colon whose muscle can no longer propel feces;
the cause of the neuromuscular failure is usually undetermined.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p50** (common — named the most common cause of *chronic* feline constipation; ordinal bucket)
- **causal-chain:** `neuromuscular lesion of colon wall → colon fails to propel → feces retained & dry → obstipation`

## .anchor

From [VCA Animal Hospitals — Constipation in Cats](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/constipation-in-cats):
> "Megacolon refers to a dilated and weak colon that causes severe constipation. Megacolon is the most common cause of constipation in cats. In this condition, the weakened muscles of the colon fail to propel fecal matter out of the colon, due to neurological impairment, problems with the muscles lining the colon, or both."

From [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-large-intestine-in-small-animals/constipation-obstipation-and-megacolon-in-small-animals):
> "some animals (usually cats) with chronic constipation or obstipation may have megacolon, likely caused by a lesion of the neuromuscular bed of the colon. The etiology of megacolon often remains undiagnosed."

## .note

megacolon is often the **end state** of long-standing constipation of any cause — a symptom that
overstayed becomes the lesion. it can be primary (idiopathic) or secondary to the causes below.
