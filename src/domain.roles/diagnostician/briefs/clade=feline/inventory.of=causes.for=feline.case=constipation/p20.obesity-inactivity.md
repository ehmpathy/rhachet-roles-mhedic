# p20.obesity-inactivity

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .cause

**obesity / inactivity / older age** — poor colonic muscle contraction and low physical activity
slow colonic transit.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p20** (a named at-risk profile; ordinal bucket)
- **causal-chain:** `obesity + low activity + age → weak colonic motility → slow transit → more water reabsorbed → dry hard stool`

## .anchor

From [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-large-intestine-in-small-animals/constipation-obstipation-and-megacolon-in-small-animals):
> "Older, overweight cats (with poor colonic muscle contraction) ... and those with previous episodes of constipation are at increased risk of constipation."

From [Catwatch Newsletter (Cornell) — From Constipation to Megacolon](https://www.catwatchnewsletter.com/health/medicine/from-constipation-to-megacolon/):
> "Older, overweight cats with poor colonic muscle contraction ... are at increased risk of constipation."

## .note

this is a **background risk multiplier** more than an acute trigger — it lowers the threshold at
which any additional hit (dehydration, reduced activity after a vet visit) produces visible
constipation.
