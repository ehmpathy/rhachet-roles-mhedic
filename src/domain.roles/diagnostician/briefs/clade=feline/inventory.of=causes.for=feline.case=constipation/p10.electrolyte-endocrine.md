# p10.electrolyte-endocrine

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .cause

**electrolyte / endocrine disturbance** — hypokalemia, hypercalcemia, hypothyroidism, or
dysautonomia disrupt the neuromuscular control that drives colonic motility.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p10** (uncommon; requires an underlying metabolic disease; ordinal bucket)
- **causal-chain:** `metabolic/endocrine derangement → impaired colonic neuromuscular control → hypomotility → retained, drying feces`

## .anchor

From [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-large-intestine-in-small-animals/constipation-obstipation-and-megacolon-in-small-animals):
> "Other diseases that affect neuromuscular control of the colon and rectum include hypothyroidism, dysautonomia, lesions of the spinal cord ... or pelvic nerves, hypokalemia, and hypercalcemia."

From [Cornell Feline Health Center — Constipation](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/constipation):
> "electrolyte disturbances, neuromuscular disease ... can predispose to constipation in cats."

## .note

these are **found on bloodwork**, not on exam — which is one reason baseline labs matter in a
constipated cat. hypokalemia in particular can be both a cause *and* a consequence of CKD.
