# p50.chronic-kidney-disease

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .cause

**chronic kidney disease (CKD)** — common in older cats; drives chronic mild dehydration via
excess urine production.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p50** (common — a named at-risk group; ordinal bucket)
- **causal-chain:** `CKD → polyuria (excess urine) → chronic mild dehydration → colon over-reabsorbs water → dry hard stool`

## .anchor

From [International Cat Care — Constipation in cats](https://icatcare.org/articles/constipation-in-cats):
> "Chronic kidney disease is common in older cats and leads to more urine being produced, so cats tend to become mildly dehydrated and this can cause constipation."

From [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-large-intestine-in-small-animals/constipation-obstipation-and-megacolon-in-small-animals):
> "cats with chronic kidney disease (that are chronically dehydrated) ... are at increased risk of constipation."

## .why it matters here

CKD is a **standing background risk** in senior cats — it makes any *additional* transient
dehydrating hit (a stressful vet visit, reduced intake, an NSAID) more likely to tip into visible
constipation. relevant to all three correlation briefs.
