# p50.dehydration

> ⚠️ **not veterinary advice — informational only.** consult a licensed veterinarian; if this looks
> like an emergency, call an emergency vet now.

## .cause

**dehydration / low water intake** — the single most modifiable driver of dry, hard stool.

## .expectation

- **probability:** **p50** (common — a frequent, well-documented driver; ordinal bucket, not a measured rate)
- **causal-chain:** `low water intake / fluid loss → colon over-reabsorbs water from retained feces → dry, hard stool → hard to pass`

## .anchor

From [International Cat Care — Constipation in cats](https://icatcare.org/articles/constipation-in-cats):
> "Water is absorbed from faeces in the colon and if cats are dehydrated (or not drinking enough), they will try to reabsorb more fluid from the colon, resulting in dry and hard faeces."

From [Washabau & Day, *Canine and Feline Gastroenterology* (PMC7152016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7152016/):
> "Sodium and water absorption serve to dehydrate the feces prior to defecation ... Perturbations in any of these functions may result in the problems of diarrhea, constipation".

## .why it ranks p50

it is both **common** and the **final common pathway** many other causes funnel through (CKD,
reduced intake, drugs). restoring hydration is the first lever in most management plans.
