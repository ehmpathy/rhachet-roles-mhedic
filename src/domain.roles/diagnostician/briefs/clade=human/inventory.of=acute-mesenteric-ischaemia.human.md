## .what

route reuses them rather than re-derives them (`rule.require.accrue-research`). every line is
bhrowser-verified against **S10 — StatPearls, Acute Mesenteric Ischemia**,

## 🔴 .the inversion — a SOFT belly is the early sign, tenderness is LATE

this is the fact most likely to be reasoned about backwards, so it leads.

> *"AMI patients typically present with abdominal pain that **does not correlate with physical exam
> findings**. **Abdominal tenderness to palpation occurs when the entire bowel wall is involved,
> which is a later presentation as necrosis begins.**"*

⇒ 🔴 **carry this as a trap on permanent record.** the inference *"the belly is tender, therefore
not AMI"* is **refuted by the source**. tenderness does not exclude AMI — it marks the point at
which necrosis has started. the absence of tenderness is the early picture, never the safe one.

> *"Findings of **abdominal pain out of proportion to a physical exam** and cardiovascular risk
> factors that predispose a patient to embolism and peripheral thrombosis should alert the
> clinician to consider AMI."*

## .the three subtypes, and they present differently

| subtype | share | the presentation, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| **arterial embolism** | *"40% to 50%"* | *"Patients with an embolic disease typically have a history of bowel emptying violently, followed by severe pain. The syndrome rapidly advances to ischemia and necrosis because collateral blood flow is limited."* |
| **arterial thrombosis** | *"25% to 30%"* | *"Thrombosis may take days or weeks to progress, with abdominal pain gradually worsening. Patients also may have a combination of diarrhea, distention, bloody stool, and, most importantly, a history of postprandial pain"* |
| **non-occlusive (NOMI)** | *"approximately 20%"* | *"NOMI progresses slowly, and the associated abdominal pain is not localized and varies in severity and consistency. These patients are critically ill ... hypotensive, and usually on vasopressor agents."* |

🔴 **the thrombotic subtype explains a diarrhoea-plus-pain picture over days.** an argument of the
form *"AMI is hyperacute, so a 4–5 day history rules it out"* is refuted by the middle row.

## .the base rate — and who it affects

> *"AMI is an uncommon but highly morbid condition, with an estimated prevalence of **approximately
> 1 per 1,000 hospital admissions**. ... AMI **most frequently affects older adults**, with a higher
> incidence observed in women and in patients with significant underlying comorbidities."*

⇒ this is the fact that keeps AMI **low-rank** even when a feature matches. it is rare, and it is
a condition of older adults with comorbidity.

## .the risk factors

| subtype | named causes |
|---|---|
| embolic | *"recent myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, and atrial fibrillation ... peripheral arterial emboli, cardiac emboli, and a ruptured or dislodged atheromatous plaque after surgery"* |
| thrombotic | *"atheromatous vascular disease (eg, atherosclerosis, aortic aneurysm, aortic dissection) and decreased cardiac output due to a secondary cause (eg, dehydration, myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure)"* |
| NOMI | *"drugs that reduce blood flow (eg, vasopressors and ergotamines), hypotension from severe medical conditions ... patients who have recently undergone major surgery"* |

⚠️ **dehydration is a named thrombotic risk factor.** so a prolonged diarrhoeal illness is not only
a differential — it is a documented exposure that raises the risk.

## .the stakes

> *"a life-threatening condition resulting from an abrupt reduction in mesenteric blood flow,
> leading to rapid progression to bowel ischemia, necrosis, sepsis, and **mortality rates
> approaching 60% to 80%**."*

## 🔴 .the workup — and why one blood draw does not settle it

> *"**CT angiography** should be pursued whenever AMI is suspected, **including in patients with
> acute kidney injury or compromised renal function**, as the consequences of a missed diagnosis
> far outweigh the risks of contrast-induced nephropathy."*

> *"Frequent, close monitoring is necessary, incorporating **serial abdominal examinations performed
> by the same clinician**, alongside laboratory assessments, eg, **serial lactic acid levels, base
> deficit, anion gap, and bicarbonate**, to detect clinical deterioration and guide timely
> intervention."*

⚠️ 🔴 **the discriminator is SERIAL, never a single value.** an assay plan that expects one lactate
to settle AMI will mis-rank it. and note the *same clinician* clause — the signal is the **change**
between exams, which is lost across a handover.

## .the differential the source itself names

> *"These include **acute colitis, ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm, bowel obstruction, diabetic
> ketoacidosis, gastrointestinal perforation, and malignancy**."*

⇒ useful in reverse: any lineup that carries AMI should also carry these six, since the source
treats them as the set AMI is confused with.
