## .what

case-neutral facts about ectopic pregnancy, held here so any route reuses
them rather than re-derives them (`rule.require.accrue-research`). every line is
bhrowser-verified against **S3 — NHS, Ectopic pregnancy**,
`https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ectopic-pregnancy/` (page last reviewed 23 Aug 2022), read
2026-09-05.

## .the base rate

> *"In the UK, around **1 in every 90 pregnancies is ectopic**. This is around 11,000 pregnancies
> a year."*

## 🔴 .the symptom window — the fact that dates a suspicion

> *"An ectopic pregnancy doesn't always cause symptoms and may only be detected during a routine
> pregnancy scan. If you do have symptoms, they tend to develop **between the 4th and 12th week of
> pregnancy**."*

⇒ **this is the number that turns a cycle report into evidence rather than into a hunch.** a
conception in the *current* cycle sits below the window and cannot yet be symptomatic; a
conception in the *preceding* cycle sits inside it.

⚠️ so a cycle report that appears to exclude a pregnancy **excludes only the cycle it covers**,
and it speaks to the cycle before it not at all. that asymmetry is the whole of the fact.

## .the symptoms

> *"Symptoms can include a combination of: a missed period and other signs of pregnancy / tummy
> pain low down on **one side** / vaginal bleeding or a brown watery discharge / pain in the tip
> of your shoulder / discomfort when peeing or pooing"*

⚠️ *"low down on one side"* is the **site** discriminator. a midline pain fits it poorly — which is
evidence, never an elimination.

## 🔴 .the emergency criteria

> *"Call 999 for an ambulance or go to your nearest accident and emergency (A&E) department
> immediately if you experience a combination of: a **sharp, sudden and intense pain** in your
> tummy / feeling very **dizzy or fainting** / feeling sick / looking very **pale**. These symptoms
> could mean that your fallopian tube has split open (ruptured)."*

## 🔴 .a negative test does NOT close the pathway

> *"Contact your GP or call NHS 111 if you have a combination of any of the above symptoms and you
> might be pregnant – **even if you haven't had a positive pregnancy test.**"*

⇒ carry this as the standing counter to any argument that de-ranks a pregnancy test, or that reads
a negative one as an exclusion. it is `absence-of-evidence`, published as policy.

## .the risk factors

> *"pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) … / previous ectopic pregnancy – the risk of having another
> ectopic pregnancy is around **10%** / previous surgery on your fallopian tubes … / **fertility
> treatment, such as IVF – taking medicine to stimulate ovulation (the release of an egg) can
> increase the risk of ectopic pregnancy** / becoming pregnant while using an intrauterine device
> (IUD) or intrauterine system (IUS) … / smoking / increasing age – the risk is highest for
> pregnant women aged over 35"*

🔴 **the ovulation-stimulation edge is the one most easily skipped.** wherever conception is actively
sought, fertility medication is plausible — and that medication is a *risk factor for the emergency*,
never merely a source of gastrointestinal side effects.

⇒ **so a medication list is not background.** it is a discriminator here, and one free question
reaches both this row and ovarian hyperstimulation.

## .the diagnostic pathway

> *"you'll usually need to do a **pregnancy test** to determine if you could have an ectopic
> pregnancy. You may be referred to a specialist early pregnancy clinic for further assessment,
> where an **ultrasound scan and blood tests** may be carried out to confirm the diagnosis."*

⇒ **the free test gates the expensive scan.** a plan that orders the scan before the test spends
on a pathway the test would have opened or closed.

## .the treatments

> *"expectant management … / medicine – an injection of a powerful medicine called **methotrexate**
> … / surgery – keyhole surgery (laparoscopy) … to remove the fertilised egg, usually along with
> the affected fallopian tube"*

> *"If you were treated with methotrexate, it's usually recommended that you wait at least
> **3 months** because the medicine could harm your baby if you become pregnant during this time."*

## .the outlook

> *"A rupture can be life threatening, but fortunately they're uncommon and treatable, if dealt
> with quickly. Deaths from ruptures are extremely rare in the UK."*

> *"Most women who have had an ectopic pregnancy will be able to get pregnant again, even if
> they've had a fallopian tube removed."*

⚠️ **carry the outlook alongside the emergency criteria.** a red flag stated without its prognosis
reads as a verdict; the pair is what makes urgency actionable rather than merely frightening.

# 🔢 the formal base rates — professional tier

> **source** · MSD Manual **Professional** Edition, *Ectopic Pregnancy*, full review May 2025,
> last updated Jul 2026 · read via bhrowser 2026-09-06 ·
> `https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/early-pregnancy-disorders/ectopic-pregnancy`

⚠️ **the professional tier was added because the whole corpus above is patient-tier NHS prose, which
publishes ADJECTIVES (*"uncommon"*, *"extremely rare"*) and no denominators.** any pass that weights
scenarios needs rates, and an adjective cannot be weighted
(`rule.require.source-tier-carries-the-quantity`).

⚠️ **every block below is verbatim.** the -ing tokens inside them are the sources' words, quoted
under `rule.require.bhrowser-citations`, never names this file authors.

## .the incidence, with its denominator

> *"Incidence of ectopic pregnancy is approximately **2/100 diagnosed pregnancies**"*
> — cited there to Van Den Eeden SK, Shan J, Bruce C, Glasser M, *Obstet Gynecol* 105(5 Pt 1):1052–1057

🔴 **the denominator is `diagnosed pregnancies`, never `women`, and never `women with abdominal
pain`.** the rate is undefined for anyone whose pregnancy status is unestablished.

## 🔴🔴 .the fertility-treatment magnitude — AMBIGUOUS, and no magnitude may be taken from it

the patient tier supplies only a bare direction — NHS: *"taking medicine to stimulate ovulation …
**can increase the risk** of ectopic pregnancy"*. the professional tier supplies the number:

> *"Heterotopic pregnancy (simultaneous ectopic and intrauterine pregnancies) occurs in only
> **1/10,000 to 30,000 pregnancies** but may be more common among women who have had **ovulation
> induction** or used assisted reproductive technologies …; **in these women, the overall reported
> ectopic pregnancy rate is 1 to 2%**"*
> — cited there to Perkins KM, Boulet SL, Kissin DM, et al, *Obstet Gynecol* 125(1):70–78, 2015

🔴🔴 **a first draft of this block set the 1–2% against the ~2% general rate and concluded the
fertility-treatment effect was small. THAT CLAIM IS WITHDRAWN**, on two independent grounds:

| sense | what "1 to 2%" counts | implication |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | **all ectopics** in ART women | ~2% vs ~2% — a small effect |
| **(b)** | **heterotopic** pregnancies in ART women | 🔴 **1–2% against a 1/10,000–30,000 baseline = a 100–600× rise** — a strongly SUPPORTED effect |

⚠️ **the sentence's grammatical subject throughout is HETEROTOPIC**, and *"but may be more common"*
is a clause about heterotopic ⇒ that favours **(b)**. the cited title (Perkins 2015, *Risk of
**ectopic** pregnancy associated with assisted reproductive technology*) favours **(a)**.

🔴 **and even under (a) the comparison is invalid**: Van Den Eeden 2015 (a managed-care population)
and Perkins 2015 (a national ART registry) differ in population, ascertainment and year. **two rates
from two denominators cannot be subtracted.**

⇒ **the risk factor keeps its cited DIRECTION and has NO established magnitude.** ⚠️ **the withdrawn
claim ran in the CALM direction** — the most costly direction to be wrong in here. the open task:
read Perkins 2015 directly, or find a source that states the ART ectopic rate unambiguously.

## 🔴🔴 .the suspicion criterion — and it OVERRIDES menstrual history

**the same MSD Professional page states this twice**, once in Diagnosis and once in Pearls & Pitfalls:

> *"Ectopic pregnancy is suspected in any female patient of reproductive age with **pelvic pain**,
> vaginal bleeding, or unexplained syncope or hemorrhagic shock, **regardless of menstrual,
> contraceptive, and sexual history**."*

> *"Suspect ectopic pregnancy in any female patient of reproductive age with pelvic pain, vaginal
> bleeding, or unexplained syncope or hemorrhagic shock, **regardless of menstrual, contraceptive,
> and sexual history and examination findings**."*

🔴🔴 **the trigger list is DISJUNCTIVE** — reproductive age plus **any one** of pelvic pain, vaginal
blood loss, or unexplained syncope. no second condition is required, and pelvic pain alone satisfies
it.

⚠️ 🔴 **and it forbids the move a reader most wants to make.** *"regardless of menstrual … history"*
means **a reported period, a tracked cycle position, and a predicted ovulation date may NOT lower
suspicion below the test threshold** — for any patient. the page names the next step itself: *"The
first step in diagnosis is a urine pregnancy test."*

⇒ 🔴 **the reason the guidance is written that way is that menstrual history fails in exactly this
mode**: an ectopic sheds the uterine wall anyway, so the blood loss is logged as a period and the
whole cycle calculation is displaced by a cycle. ⇒ **a cycle-tracker app inherits that error rather
than a catch of it** — its prediction is computed FROM the logged event, so it is never a second
source.

⇒ **the transferable rule: where a guideline says *regardless of X*, X is usually the datum that
fails silently.** the phrase is a warn about a specific failure mode, never boilerplate caution.

## .the rupture window — a temporal bound

> *"The anatomic structure containing the fetus usually ruptures after about **6 to 16 weeks**."*

🔴 **the window is what a reported cycle position gets weighed against**: a presentation only weeks
from a reported period sits well outside 6–16 weeks, so **ruptured** ectopic de-ranks on the
reported timeline.

⚠️⚠️ **and that is an ARGUMENT, never an elimination** — three independent reasons, each of which
holds case-neutrally:

1. **the cycle position is reported, never verified**, so the timeline's own input is unconfirmed
2. the NHS page above: *"even if you haven't had a positive pregnancy test"* — a **negative test**
   does not close the pathway, so a **reported period** certainly does not
3. `rule.require.superposition-until-elimination` — an argument is not an elimination

⇒ **the window de-ranks RUPTURED ectopic specifically and leaves UNRUPTURED ectopic untouched.** the
two have different windows and the same test settles both. ⚠️ **that asymmetry is the trap** — a
reader who de-ranks on the window alone drops the wrong half.

## .the test characteristic — why the pregnancy test is the gate

> *"the first step in diagnosis is a **urine pregnancy test, which is approximately 99% sensitive**
> for pregnancy (ectopic and otherwise)"*

⚠️ **hold this against the NHS line already accrued** — *"even if you haven't had a positive
pregnancy test"*. **both are cited, and they are in tension**: 99% sensitivity is high, and the NHS
still refuses to let a negative close the pathway. ⇒ the reconciliation is that **99% is not 100%,
and the miss is a fatal-if-missed condition** — which is why policy outruns the test characteristic.

## .the severity rate

> *"In the United States in 2018, the mortality rate due to ectopic pregnancies was **0.8 deaths per
> 100,000 live births**"* — cited there to Hoyert DL, Miniño AM, *National Vital Statistics Reports*
> vol 69 no 2, 2020

## .the risk factors, professional tier — the two most easily skipped

> *"Factors that **particularly** increase risk … **Assisted reproductive technologies used in
> current pregnancy**, particularly with history of tubal infertility or multiple embryo transfer"*
> · *"Other risk factors … **Infertility**"*

🔴 **`infertility` is itself a listed risk factor, independent of any treatment for it.** so an
active conception attempt does not settle the question — whether it meets `infertility` turns on
**duration**, which an intake rarely captures.

⇒ **the transferable point: a duration question that reads as a timeline detail can be gating a RISK
FACTOR.** *"how long have you been trying?"* looks like calendar bookkeeping and is a risk-factor ask.

> *"approximately **53%** in pregnancies in current IUD users"*

⚠️ carried for completeness; **inapplicable unless an IUD is established** — and note that an intake
which never asks about contraception cannot even reach this row.
