## 🔴 .WORSE ON PRESS — formal, and it is an emergency criterion

three separate NHS pages put tenderness-on-touch in a **999 / A&E** list, never a GP list.

| the criterion, verbatim | page |
|---|---|
| *"it hurts when you touch your stomach"* | NHS — Stomach ache, `https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/stomach-ache/` |
| *"it's painful to touch your tummy"* | NHS — Stomach ulcer, `https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stomach-ulcer/` |
| *"pelvic pain that's severe, getting worse, or **hurts when you move or touch the area**"* | NHS — PID, `https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pelvic-inflammatory-disease-pid/` |

⇒ 🔴 **this modifier is the reason a lay-shaped complaint can be an emergency-shaped one.**

⚠️ 🔴 **the honest bound: DEGREE cannot be judged remotely.** the NHS criteria do not distinguish
mild discomfort on firm press from guard and rigidity. **a text exchange cannot settle degree of
tenderness; a hand on the abdomen can.** so this modifier raises acuity, and it does not by itself
establish it.

### the counter-case that must travel with it

worse-on-press is **also** listed for appendicitis at a non-emergency tier:

> *"The pain may feel worse when you move, cough or **press on the area**."* — NHS, Appendicitis,
> `https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/appendicitis/`

⇒ so the modifier is **not specific**. it appears at both tiers, which is exactly why it cannot be
read alone.

### 🔴 the inversion for mesenteric ischaemia — tenderness is LATE, never absent-therefore-safe

> *"Abdominal tenderness to palpation occurs when the entire bowel wall is involved, **which is a
> later presentation as necrosis begins**."* — StatPearls, Acute Mesenteric Ischemia,
> `https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431068/`

⇒ carry as a trap: *"tender, so not ischaemia"* is refuted. and its mirror, *"soft belly, so
safe"*, is refuted harder — a soft belly is the **early** ischaemic picture.

massage at all.** that absence is recorded rather than filled by inference.

⚠️ **the lay reports are PARAPHRASED here, never quoted, and no thread is linked.** a post-level

### the adjacent FORMAL discriminator, which is the one to actually ask

relief after passage of stool or wind **does** have a formal source, for the diverticular family:

> *"tummy pain that **gets worse after you eat, and gets better after you poo or fart**"* — NHS,
> Diverticular disease, `https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/diverticular-disease-and-diverticulitis/`

⇒ so the *"better after wind"* half is formally attested; the *"better on rub"* half is not.
**a question about wind and stool is worth more than a question about rub**, because only the
first has a source behind it.

### 🔴🔴 the FIRST formal traction on the better-on-rub half

⚠️ **this file's claim that no formal source speaks to relief-on-rub still holds literally** — MSD
does not discuss massage. **but it does publish the picture that relief-on-rub is inconsistent
with**, which is indirect formal traction where no direct source exists:

> **source** · MSD Manual **Professional** Edition, *Acute Abdominal Pain* (rev. Jul 2024), read via
> bhrowser 2026-09-06

> *"**Severe pain in a patient with a silent abdomen who is lying as still as possible suggests
> peritonitis**…"*

> *"**Guarding is an involuntary contraction of the abdominal muscles** that is slightly slower and
> more sustained than the rapid, voluntary flinch exhibited by sensitive or anxious patients."*

🔴 **a patient who obtains relief by rubbing their own abdomen is not the patient in either
sentence.** the peritonitis posture is stillness; guarding is *involuntary* and therefore not
something a rub can ease.

⚠️⚠️ **and this changes NO PART of the rule below it.** three bounds, each independent:

1. **it is an argument, never an elimination** (`rule.require.superposition-until-elimination`) —
   and it argues against **peritonitis specifically**, not against appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy,
   or any unruptured row
2. **peritonitis is a LATE state.** early appendicitis and an unruptured ectopic both precede it, so
   its absence de-ranks the COMPLICATION and leaves the CONDITION untouched — the same shape as the
   ruptured-vs-unruptured split for ectopic pregnancy
3. 🔴 **it runs in the CALM direction**, which this file already names as the dangerous one: *"the
   benign-shaped feature is the one a frightened person reaches for."*

⇒ **carry it as a de-rank of PERITONITIS ONLY**, stated in the same breath as the three bounds. ⚠️
**never let it travel alone** — detached from its bounds it becomes exactly the *"it feels better
when I rub it, so it's probably fine"* inference this file exists to refuse.

## 🔴 .the rule these two modifiers teach

> **a benign-shaped modifier does not cancel an emergency-shaped one.**

when worse-on-press and better-on-rub appear **in the same patient**, the effect set is **mixed**,
and mixed is the answer. per `rule.require.superposition-until-elimination`, an argument is not an
elimination: relief on rub is an argument for a gas-type cause and **eliminates no row**.

⚠️ the failure mode this guards against is real and directional — **the benign-shaped feature is
the one a frightened person reaches for.** the pull is toward *"but it feels better when I rub it,
so it's probably fine"*, and the literature above gives that inference no support.

## .the normal-course baseline, so a modifier is judged against a floor

> *"Most people recover from appendicitis in 1 to 2 weeks."* — NHS, Appendicitis

NHS — Stomach ache leads with a **symptom → possible condition** table whose four entries include
**trapped wind** and **indigestion**. that table is the calibration floor a severity-weighted
lineup lacks.

> ⚠️ the table is organised by **symptom**, never by **frequency** — NHS does not rank these four
> or call them the commonest, and this file makes no such claim.

⇒ a lineup with no benign rows will read every modifier as evidence for a severe row. keep the
common causes present, precisely so the modifiers have somewhere ordinary to land.
