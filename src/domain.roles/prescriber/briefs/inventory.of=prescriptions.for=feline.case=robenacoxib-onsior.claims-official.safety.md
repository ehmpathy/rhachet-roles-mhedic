> from public sources to the best of our knowledge; it carries **no guarantee of outcome** and is
> **not a substitute for a licensed veterinarian** who has examined your cat and knows its full
> history, labs, and other medications. Onsior (robenacoxib) is prescription-only. if your cat
> shows signs of a drug reaction, contact your veterinarian or an emergency veterinary service now.

## .what

is Onsior (robenacoxib) **safe for cats**? short answer from the evidence found: it is an
**FDA-approved, prescription** feline NSAID with a comparatively wide safety margin and a
COX-2-selective design, and in controlled trials it was **well tolerated** — but it is **not risk
free**, is only US-approved for **short (≤3-day) post-operative** use, and is **contraindicated or
higher-risk** in specific cats (dehydrated, on diuretics, existing kidney/heart/liver disease,
pregnant/nursing, very young/small, NSAID-intolerant).

## .approved use and who may prescribe

From [FDA/DailyMed Onsior tablet label (Elanco)](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4):
> "Federal law restricts this drug to use by or on the order of a licensed veterinarian."

> "ONSIOR tablets are indicated for the control of postoperative pain and inflammation associated with orthopedic surgery, ovariohysterectomy and castration, in cats ≥ 5.5 lbs (2.5 kg) and ≥ 4 months of age; for up to a maximum of 3 days."

> "The dose of ONSIOR tablets is 0.45 mg/lb (1 mg/kg) orally once daily, for a maximum of 3 days ... Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration consistent with individual response."

Note: EU labeling additionally allows **chronic** musculoskeletal use; in the **US** the label is
limited to ≤3 days, and longer/osteoarthritis use is **off-label**. From
[VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/robenacoxib):
> "In the US, the use of robenacoxib in cats and dogs to treat inflammation due to musculoskeletal disorders is 'off label'."

## .safety margin (healthy cats)

From [Lees et al. 2022, pharmacology review (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "Robenacoxib has a wide safety margin; in healthy laboratory animals daily oral doses ... eight‐fold (cat, 6 weeks) ... higher than recommended clinical doses were well tolerated."

From [King et al. 2016, injectable RCT (PMC5041542)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5041542/):
> "There were no significant changes from baseline with robenacoxib in hepatic, hematological or renal clinical pathology variables."

From [Heit et al. 2020, interchangeable-use safety (PMC7520961)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7520961/):
> "All cats were in good health through study termination and there were no serious adverse events during the study. ... Treatment-related abnormalities were of low occurrence at all doses and included injection site changes ... and prolongation of the QT interval."

— bhrowser verified 2026-08-08

## .safety over longer/chronic use and in kidney disease

From [King et al. 2021, *J Vet Intern Med* — pooled CMSD safety, 449 cats (PMC8478032)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478032/):
> "Robenacoxib was not associated with increased risk of AEs compared to placebo when administered for 4 to 12 weeks to cats with CMSD."

> "Serum creatinine concentrations were higher during robenacoxib administration compared to placebo (+4.36 μmol/L, 95% confidence interval 0.21‐8.50), but no related adverse clinical effects were detected. In the subgroup of 126 cats with evidence of chronic kidney disease, the relative risk of at least 1 AE (robenacoxib/placebo) was 1.09".

> "The generalizability of the results to general practice is limited by the fact that cases with severe and uncontrolled concomitant diseases were not included."

## .who is at higher risk / contraindications

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4):
> "ONSIOR tablets should not be used in cats that have a hypersensitivity to robenacoxib or known intolerance to NSAIDs."

> "As a class, cyclo-oxygenase inhibitory NSAIDs may be associated with gastrointestinal, renal, and hepatic toxicity. ... Patients at greatest risk for adverse events are those that are dehydrated, on concomitant diuretic therapy, or those with existing renal, cardiovascular, and/or hepatic dysfunction."

> "The use of ONSIOR tablets has not been evaluated in cats younger than 4 months of age and weighing less than 5.5 lbs, cats used for breeding, or in pregnant or lactating cats."

> "The use of ONSIOR in cats with cardiac disease has not been studied. ONSIOR has been shown to prolong the QT interval."

From [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/robenacoxib):
> "Robenacoxib SHOULD NOT BE USED in pets that: are allergic to it or other NSAIDs / have gastrointestinal ulcers / are pregnant or nursing / are younger than four months old"

> "Robenacoxib SHOULD BE USED WITH EXTREME CAUTION, or not at all, in pets that: are dehydrated / are on diuretic therapy / have existing severe renal, heart, or liver disease"

— bhrowser verified 2026-08-08

## .drug interactions

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4):
> "Do not administer ONSIOR tablets or injection in conjunction with any other oral or injectable NSAID or corticosteroid."

> "The use of robenacoxib with other drugs shown to prolong the QT interval is not recommended. Commonly used drugs that prolong the QT interval include antihistamines, prokinetic drugs, and certain anesthetic drugs."

> "Concurrent administration of potentially nephrotoxic drugs should be carefully approached and monitored."

From [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/robenacoxib), medications of concern include:
> "cyclosporine (Atopica®) / furosemide (Lasix®) / selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) ... / tricyclic antidepressants".

On the NSAID + ACE-inhibitor + diuretic ("triple whammy") renal concern, from
[King et al. 2016 — benazepril/robenacoxib GFR study (PMC4917992)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917992/):
> "The combination of benazepril and robenacoxib was well tolerated and either increased or had a neutral effect on GFR in healthy cats without or with concomitant furosemide. ... It is recommended to test the efficacy and safety of the combined use ... in cats with clinical disease, notably proteinuric chronic kidney disease."

(that reassurance is from **healthy** cats; the authors explicitly flag that diseased cats are untested.)

## .deeper safety-signal analysis (from the review + trials)

The class worries about coxibs are GI/wound-healing harm and cardiovascular (MI/stroke) events.
From [Lees et al. 2022 (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "Potential safety concerns of coxib NSAIDs are wound healing inhibition (including pre‐existing gastrointestinal ulcers) and increased risk of myocardial ischaemia or stroke. Signals for these effects were not detected in robenacoxib safety and clinical studies in cats and dogs".

> "No safety signals for robenacoxib were detected in safety and clinical studies in cats and dogs for wound healing, the gastrointestinal tract and kidney tissues ... no issues related to healing were reported when robenacoxib was administered up to 15 days in dogs and cats undergoing fracture repair".

quantified, the long-term feline harm signal was small and not significant — from
[King et al. 2021 (PMC8478032)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478032/):
> "All adverse events 106 (47.7%) [robenacoxib] ... 93 (41.0%) [placebo] ... 1.15 [relative risk] 0.93‐1.43" — and "Increased blood urea nitrogen or creatinine 1 (0.5%) ... 0 (0.0%)".

> **but note the population limits:** "No IRIS stage 4 CKD cases were included because that was an exclusion criterion." the reassuring CKD data covers **stages 1–3**, not end-stage kidney disease.

## .the "triple whammy" (NSAID + ACE-inhibitor + diuretic) — feline data

From [King et al. 2016, GFR study (PMC4917992)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917992/):
> "The combination of benazepril and robenacoxib was well tolerated and either increased or had a neutral effect on GFR in healthy cats without or with concomitant furosemide."

Lees' review adds the crucial boundary — from [Lees et al. 2022 (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "These findings in healthy animals did not identify increased acute kidney injury risk with combined benazepril and robenacoxib administration. However, a similar safety level might not apply in dehydrated animals or in canine and feline cardiac or kidney disease patients."

→ the renal reassurance is **healthy-cat only**; in dehydration or real kidney/heart disease the
triple-whammy risk is untested and the label's high-risk cautions govern.

## .recommended precautions from the label

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4):
> "All cats should undergo a thorough history and physical examination before the initiation of NSAID therapy. Appropriate laboratory tests should be conducted to establish hematological and serum biochemical baseline data prior to administration of an NSAID."

> "When using NSAIDS such as ONSIOR, the use of fluid therapy during surgery is recommended to decrease potential renal complications. ... Stop administration of ONSIOR if appetite decreases or if the cat becomes lethargic."

## .bottom line

- **Yes, it is an approved, generally well-tolerated feline NSAID** used routinely for peri-operative
  pain, with a COX-2-selective, inflammation-targeting design and a wide margin in healthy cats.
- **But safety is conditional**: short-course by the US label, blood work first, avoid in
  NSAID-intolerant / GI-ulcer / pregnant-nursing / <4 mo / <5.5 lb cats, and use extreme caution in
  dehydration, diuretic use, or existing kidney/heart/liver disease. Never combine with other
  NSAIDs or steroids. A veterinarian must make the call for a specific cat.
