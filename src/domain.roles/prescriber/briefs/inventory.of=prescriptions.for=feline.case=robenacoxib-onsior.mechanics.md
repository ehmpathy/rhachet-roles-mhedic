## .what

how robenacoxib (brand **Onsior**, Elanco) works in cats: a **coxib-class NSAID** that
selectively blocks the **COX-2** enzyme while largely sparing **COX-1**, and that concentrates and
persists at sites of inflammation rather than in the blood.

this is a **veterinary** drug. it is placed in the prescriber briefs as a worked example of a
drug-mechanism reference; it is not human-medicine guidance.

## .how it works — COX-2 selectivity

NSAIDs relieve pain, inflammation and fever by inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX), which makes
prostaglandins. COX-1 is constitutive and protective (gastric lining, renal blood flow, platelets);
COX-2 is mostly induced at sites of pain and inflammation. Coxibs aim to hit COX-2 and spare COX-1.

From [Lees et al. 2022, *J Vet Pharmacol Ther* — pharmacology review (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "Robenacoxib inhibits the cyclooxygenase (COX)‐2 isoform of COX selectively (in vitro IC50 ratios COX‐1:COX‐2, 129:1 in dogs, 32:1 in cats)."

From [Heit et al. 2020, *BMC Vet Res* (PMC7520961)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7520961/):
> "The coxib class of NSAIDs selectively inhibits COX-2 isoforms ... Robenacoxib is the only coxib licensed for feline use".

From [King et al. 2016, *BMC Vet Res* — injectable RCT (PMC5041542)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5041542/):
> "Robenacoxib is a coxib class NSAID that demonstrates high selectivity for the cyclo-oxygenase-2 enzyme".

— bhrowser verified 2026-08-08

## .how it works — tissue selectivity ("targets inflammation")

Robenacoxib is highly protein-bound and short-lived in blood, yet works once-daily because it
lingers where inflammation is.

From [Lees et al. 2022 (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "The pharmacokinetic (PK) profile of robenacoxib is characterized by a high degree of binding to plasma proteins (>98%) ... the terminal half‐life in blood (<2 h) is short ... Robenacoxib concentrates in inflamed tissues, and clinical efficacy is achieved with once‐daily dosing, despite the short blood terminal half‐life. ... Excretion is principally in the bile (65% in dogs and 72% in cats)."

From [Elanco Onsior product page](https://my.elanco.com/us/onsior):
> "Onsior, a highly protein-bound NSAID, persists longer at the site of inflammation than in the blood because inflammatory exudate is rich in plasma proteins ... The mean blood T1/2 is approximately 1.7 hrs for Onsior Tablets for Cats."

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4) (Clinical Pharmacology, cats):
> "peak blood concentrations are attained rapidly with a median Tmax of 0.5 h ... The systemic mean bioavailability of robenacoxib tablets was 49% without food ... is highly bound to plasma proteins (>99%)."

From [King et al. 2012, *ISRN Vet Sci* — oral RCT (PMC3658645)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3658645/):
> "a wide safety margin in healthy cats which is attributed to its combination of high specificity for the cyclooxygenase (COX)-2 enzyme plus selective distribution and persistence at sites of inflammation".

## .feline pharmacokinetics (species specifics)

From [Lees et al. 2022 (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "Cat PK data ... Tmax (oral 0.5 h, SC 1 h) was short. The volume of distribution at steady state (190 ml/kg) and body clearance (0.44 L/kg/h) were relatively low."

> "The body clearance of robenacoxib was moderate (0.81 L/kg/h) in dogs and low (0.44 L/kg/h) in cats. The difference might reflect reduced glucuronide conjugation capacity in the cat."

> "The excretion of IV administered 14C‐radiolabelled robenacoxib was primarily in faeces ... 72.5% in cats, consistent with elimination in bile following hepatic metabolism."

(the **biliary/faecal** elimination route matters: it means renal excretion of the parent drug is
low, but hepatic metabolism carries the load — relevant to the hepatic-caution flag in the safety
brief. the cat's reduced glucuronidation is the classic reason cats tolerate few NSAIDs.)

## .why it is comparatively safe (mechanistic)

From [Lees et al. 2022 (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "A likely explanation for the lack of detectable robenacoxib toxicity at these dosages is short exposure time in the central compartment, leading to a relatively short duration of COX‐1 or COX‐2 inhibition in highly perfused organs, including the gastrointestinal tract and kidney."

so the safety story is **two-part**: COX-2 *selectivity* + *short systemic dwell time* spare the
gut and kidney, while inflammation-site persistence keeps the analgesic effect.

## .caveat

the label repeatedly notes the clinical relevance of the in-vitro COX-2 selectivity and the
inflammation-persistence data is not fully established ("The clinical relevance of this data has
not been shown"). selectivity reduces, but does not remove, class NSAID risks — see the safety and
adverse-effect briefs.
