> substitute for a licensed veterinarian**. if your cat shows any sign below, stop the drug and
> contact your veterinarian or an emergency veterinary service now.

## .what

the **negative side effects** of Onsior (robenacoxib) in cats — from controlled field trials, the
FDA label's post-approval surveillance, and veterinary references. Most are mild and reversible on
withdrawal, but **serious NSAID reactions (kidney, liver, GI, neurologic) can occur without
warning and have, rarely, been fatal.**

## .most common (label summary)

From [FDA/DailyMed Onsior tablet label (Elanco)](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4):
> "The most commonly reported adverse reactions were surgical site bleeding, infected surgery sites, lethargy, vomiting and inappetance. Changes in the clinical pathology values were not considered clinically significant."

## .controlled field study (249 cats: 167 robenacoxib vs 82 placebo)

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4) — "Adverse Reactions in the Postoperative Pain Field Study" (counts robenacoxib vs placebo, verbatim):

| adverse reaction | ONSIOR (n=167) | placebo (n=82) |
|------------------|:---:|:---:|
| Incision site bleeding | 7 | 1 |
| Incision site infection | 6 | 2 |
| Inappetance, weight loss | 4 | 2 |
| Decreased activity, lethargy | 4 | 1 |
| Vomiting | 4 | 1 |
| Cystitis, hematuria | 3 | 0 |
| Hematachezia, diarrhea | 3 | 1 |
| Hair loss, excoriation, bruising | 2 | 0 |
| Respiratory, cardiac arrest | 1 | 0 |
| Incoordination, weakness | 1 | 1 |
| Death | 0 | 1 |

(the label notes: "Cats may have experienced more than one of these signs during the study.")

In the two pivotal feline RCTs, overall adverse-sign frequency was **similar to placebo**:

From [King et al. 2012, oral RCT (PMC3658645)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3658645/):
> "Frequency of reported adverse clinical signs, hematology, serum chemistry and urinalysis variables, and body weight changes were similar between groups."

From [King et al. 2016, injectable RCT (PMC5041542)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5041542/):
> "The most frequently reported adverse events were incision site infection/dehiscence, bleeding, vomiting, decreased appetite and lethargy. ... There were no significant changes from baseline with robenacoxib in hepatic, hematological or renal clinical pathology variables."

## .post-approval surveillance (real-world, 2015)

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4), Post Approval Experience — signs "in decreasing order of reporting frequency":
> "Anorexia, depression/ lethargy, vomiting, elevated BUN, elevated creatinine, renal insufficiency/ failure, diarrhea, weight loss, dehydration."

> "In some cases, death has been reported. Some of these cases involved patients that developed renal failure/renal insufficiency."

From [Elanco Onsior product page](https://my.elanco.com/us/onsior) (tablets, cats):
> "The most common adverse events are anorexia, depression, vomiting, elevated BUN, elevated creatinine, and renal insufficiency/failure."

## .serious "stop and call the vet" signs

From [VCA Animal Hospitals — Robenacoxib](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/robenacoxib):
> "SERIOUS ADVERSE REACTIONS ASSOCIATED WITH THIS DRUG MAY OCCUR WITHOUT WARNING AND COULD, IN RARE CASES, LEAD TO DEATH. If any of the following signs develop, stop giving robenacoxib and contact your veterinarian immediately: yellowing of the skin or gums / changes in the frequency or amount of water consumption or urination / seizures / abnormal behavior / incoordination / weakness / severe vomiting or diarrhea".

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4), Information for Cat Owners:
> "Adverse reactions may include vomiting, diarrhea, decreased appetite, dark or tarry stools, increased water consumption, increased urination, anemia, yellowing of gums, skin or white of the eye due to jaundice, lethargy, incoordination, seizure, or behavioral changes. ... The vast majority of patients with drug related adverse reactions have recovered when the signs are recognized, the drug is withdrawn, and veterinary care, if appropriate, is initiated."

## .specific organ-system signals

- **Neurologic** — from the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4):
  > "Occurrences of seizures, ataxia, and nystagmus have been associated with the use of ONSIOR."
  (the label links this to the γ-lactam metabolite/degradant: "Neurologic signs have been associated with the use of ß lactam drugs; it is unknown if the lactam in robenacoxib may cause similar neurologic signs".)

- **Cardiac (QT)** — from [Heit et al. 2020 (PMC7520961)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7520961/):
  > "Treatment-related abnormalities ... included ... prolongation of the QT interval."
  the label adds the clinical consequence is uncertain: "the associated risk of developing ventricular arrhythmia is unknown."

- **Renal (long-term signal)** — from [King et al. 2021 (PMC8478032)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478032/):
  > "Serum creatinine concentrations were higher during robenacoxib administration compared to placebo (+4.36 μmol/L ...), but no related adverse clinical effects were detected."

— bhrowser verified 2026-08-08

## .rates in the controlled trials (deeper read)

**Injectable RCT (349 cats)** — from [King et al. 2016 (PMC5041542)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5041542/), the notable surgical-site signals:
> "Incision site infection, dehiscence 9 [5.2%] ... 0 [placebo]" and "Urinary tract infection 2 [1.1%] ... 0".

**Long-term CMSD pooled safety (449 cats, 4–12 wks)** — from [King et al. 2021 (PMC8478032)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478032/):
> "All adverse events 106 (47.7%) ... 93 (41.0%) ... 1.15 ... 0.93‐1.43".

so even over weeks of dosing the **overall** adverse-event rate sat near placebo (47.7% vs 41.0%,
relative risk 1.15, confidence interval crossing 1) — i.e. no statistically significant excess,
though the point estimate is slightly higher.

**Perspective from the review** — from [Lees et al. 2022 (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "robenacoxib produces minimal adverse effects, even at high dosages, in healthy rats, dogs and cats."
(healthy-animal, high-dose lab context — real-world diseased cats can differ; see safety brief.)

## .pattern

mild/expected: GI upset (vomiting, diarrhea, inappetence), lethargy, surgical-site bleeding/infection.
serious/rare: renal insufficiency/failure, hepatic injury (jaundice), GI ulceration/bleeding
(dark/tarry stools), neurologic signs (seizure, ataxia), QT prolongation — any of these warrants
immediate drug withdrawal and veterinary contact. Reversibility is common **if caught early**.
