## .what

**does it actually work in cats?** the controlled-trial evidence for robenacoxib (Onsior)
controlling post-operative pain/inflammation in cats — the efficacy magnitudes the abstracts only
summarize.

the primary efficacy endpoint in the pivotal feline trials is **need for rescue analgesia**
(a cat needing extra pain relief = treatment failure). lower rescue = better.

## .oral tablet RCT (249 cats — King et al. 2012)

From [King et al. 2012, *ISRN Vet Sci* (PMC3658645)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3658645/):
> "Total 27 (16.5%) ... 137 (83.5%)" (robenacoxib rescued vs not) versus "Total 37 (46.3%) ... 43 (53.7%)" (placebo).

> "The risk of receiving rescue therapy was significantly (P < 0.0001) lower with robenacoxib."

i.e. **~16.5% of robenacoxib cats needed rescue vs ~46.3% on placebo.**

## .injectable RCT (349 cats — King et al. 2016)

From [King et al. 2016, *BMC Vet Res* (PMC5041542)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5041542/):
> "Significantly (P = 0.037) fewer cats administered robenacoxib received additional analgesia rescue therapy (34 of 173, 19.7 %) compared to cats given placebo (73 of 175, 41.7 %). The percentage of treatment success was therefore 80.3 % with robenacoxib and 58.3 % with placebo."

> "the log-rank, Cox-Tarone and Gehan-Breslow tests were all highly significant (P < 0.0001) in favor of the robenacoxib group."

Most rescues happened early: "77/107 (72 %) at ≤ 5 h and 91/107 (85 %) at ≤ 8 h" post-extubation —
consistent with the acute post-op pain window.

## .independent synthesis

From [Lees et al. 2022 review (PMC9541287)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541287/):
> "The overall conclusion is that robenacoxib is effective for the selected indications with a treatment effect size comparable to reference NSAIDs."

But note the evidence-quality caveat: independent (non-sponsor) feline studies are small —
> "Seven field studies have been conducted by groups independent of the sponsor company ... all were small‐scale (n = 5–30 per group)."

## .onset (why it works fast enough for surgery)

From the [FDA/DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=922d3235-085e-454f-9535-44ef4bca12f4):
> "robenacoxib had analgesic, anti-inflammatory and anti-pyretic actions with a rapid onset of action (0.5 h)."

From [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/robenacoxib):
> "This medication will take effect quickly, in about one to two hours".

From [Elanco Onsior page](https://my.elanco.com/us/onsior):
> "Effective at controlling the postoperative pain and inflammation associated with orthopedic surgery, ovariohysterectomy, and castration in cats."

## .generics carry the same efficacy

From [FDA CVM Update (Jan 2026)](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/cvm-updates/fda-approves-first-generic-robenacoxib-tablet-postoperative-pain-inflammation-cats):
> "The FDA determined that Robencoxib tablets are bioequivalent to the brand name product."

so an FDA-approved generic robenacoxib tablet is expected to deliver the same efficacy as brand Onsior.

## .bottom line

across two pivotal RCTs (249 oral, 349 injectable), robenacoxib **roughly halved (or more) the
proportion of cats needing rescue analgesia** vs placebo, with a rapid onset — an effect size an
independent review calls "comparable to reference NSAIDs." caveat: much of the feline evidence base
is sponsor-run or small-scale.
