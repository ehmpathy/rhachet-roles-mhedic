# mechanism.ugt1a6.glucuronidation-loss

## .what

cats carry a **pseudogene of UGT1A6** and a reduced UGT1A family — a loss of hepatic glucuronidation capacity for many xenobiotics.

## .clade

**felidae.** a lineage-specific loss that arose in Felidae *after* the split from the lineage that leads to humans.

## .relation-to-human

**DIVERGENT — this is the divergence point.** humans retain functional UGT1A6. cross-species *safety* insight does not transfer here.

## .divergence-point

- **where:** Felidae, post-split from the human lineage
- **probable cause:** relaxed selection under a strict-hypercarnivore diet with low plant-phenolic load

## .role-in-host-pharmacology

this is **host** pharmacology, not target biology. it governs which drugs a cat can clear:

- drugs that lean on glucuronidation (paracetamol, aspirin) → dangerous in cats
- drugs cleared by other routes (robenacoxib: hepatic oxidation + biliary excretion) → sidestep this bottleneck

## .transfer

do **not** transfer human or canine safety data across this divergence; require feline-specific data.
