# domain.term.choice.reason: excerpt

## .etymology

`excerpt` — latin *excerpere*, "to pluck out." it names **a part plucked from a whole**, which is
the point: the word carries its own part-of relation, so *"the excerpt"* already implies *"of a
larger source."*

chosen over:

| rejected | why |
|---|---|
| `snippet` | informal, and carries no sense that a whole surrounds it |
| `extract` | overloaded — this repo also extracts data from a page (the capture stage). one word, two senses |
| `passage` | names a unit of the SOURCE, not a unit an artifact took. an author writes a passage; a researcher takes an excerpt |
| `span` | a **measure**, not an object. it names how much, never what. useful in the phrase *"the excerpt's scope"*, wrong as the noun |

## .disputes

### dispute: span — raised 2026-09-06 — status: RESOLVED (keep `excerpt`)

- raised.by = a correlation stone's third self-review ⚠️ (origin route removed — `rule.forbid.pii`)
- claim = the review prose repeatedly reached for *"the wrong SPAN"*, contrasted against *"the
  wrong SOURCE"*. the pair is memorable and reads well
- counter = `span` names a **measure**. it cannot hold the object itself, so an artifact would still
  need a second word for the passage — synonym sprawl by construction. and the review's own
  contrast is better served by the two **properties** (`attribution` vs `scope`) than by two nouns
- resolution = keep `excerpt` as the noun; record `span` forbidden as a noun for the passage. the
  contrast is stated as **attribution vs scope**, both properties *of* an excerpt

## .evidence

### 🔴 the term was coined to repair an over-reach, and the over-reach was mine

`rule.require.excerpt-carries-the-qualifier` was authored 2026-09-06 and its first draft claimed:

> *"both are the same failure of **citation scope**."*

⚠️ **that is false, and `rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name` catches it.** the two instances the
word had to cover:

| instance | what was wrong | does `scope` cover it? |
|---|---|---|
| a quote credited to the one thread that reports the **opposite** of what it was cited for | the **pointer** | 🔴 **no** — the words taken were fine; the credit was wrong |
| right thread, right author, **half the post** — the half that bounded the claim was left out | the **extent** | ✅ yes |

⇒ **`scope` fits one row and was stretched over two** — the *too narrow* failure mode, applied as
if it were the genus. the true genus is **citation fidelity**; `attribution` and `scope` are its two
differentia, and both are properties of an **excerpt**.

### the instances the term must cover

1. a quote taken from a post that qualified itself, with the qualifier dropped
2. two quotes from one post, with the gap between them unread
3. a quote whose credited source does not hold it
4. a quote faithful in words, source, and extent — the case that must PASS

⇒ `excerpt` names the object in all four; `attribution` and `scope` part 3 from 1–2; row 4 is the
control that proves the terms discriminate rather than merely find fault.

## .invariants

- an excerpt is **strictly less** than its source. a whole-source quote is not an excerpt
- an excerpt has **exactly one** attribution. two candidate sources means the attribution is unsettled
- ⚠️ **an excerpt may be verbatim, well-attributed, and still defective** — when its scope drops a
  qualifier. ⇒ **verbatim is not fidelity**, and that is the invariant this term exists to carry
