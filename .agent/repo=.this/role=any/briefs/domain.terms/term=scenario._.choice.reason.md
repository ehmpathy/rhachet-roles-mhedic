# domain.term.choice.reason: scenario

## .etymology

`scenario` = one played-out course of events. in decision theory a "scenario" is one state of the
world under uncertainty — exactly the sense here: one way the patient's world could actually be. it
carries no claim of truth or likelihood on its own; the rank supplies that.

chosen over:
- **`hypothesis`** — implies a claim advanced *for test*, singular and truth-oriented. a scenario is
  a *state held in superposition* — many coexist, weighted, until a test eliminates one. "hypothesis"
  also collides with the everyday "the diagnosis is a hypothesis" hedge, which is about the whole
  read, not one branch.
- **`possibility`** — already rejected in the hazard/alert round (too vague, no structure). a
  scenario is a structured object (chain + package), not a bare "maybe."
- **`candidate`** — reads as a contest with one winner; the superposition keeps many live at once.
- **`differential`** — names the *set* (the differential diagnosis / lineup), not one member. a
  scenario is one entry in the differential, so the two are different grains, not synonyms.

## .evidence

- discovery: the superposition frame (`define.diagnosis-superposition`) required a word for the basis
  state — the unit that is enumerated, ranked, eliminated, and packaged. "scenario" is the
  decision-theory term of art for a state-of-the-world under uncertainty.
- the route composes it at every phase: `scenario.enumeration` (list them),
  `scenario.disentangle` (weight them), `scenario.elimination.via.assay` (rule them out),
  `scenario.rerank.via.redteam` (refine their rank), `scenario.superposition` (package the survivors).
