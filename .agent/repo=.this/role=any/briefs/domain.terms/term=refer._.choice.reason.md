# domain.term.choice.reason: refer

## .etymology

from latin *referre* — *re-* (back) + *ferre* (to carry): to carry a matter onward to
another who can act on it. the clinical sense inherits this exactly: the referrer carries
the patient's need onward to the care that fits it. a domain expert recognizes "refer" and
"referral" with no gloss — it is the standard word for the onward clinical handoff.

## .disputes

### dispute: recommend — raised 2026-08-14 — status: RESOLVED (keep `refer`)
- raised.by  = mechanic
- claim      = "recommend" is the softer, everyday word a patient would hear.
- counter    = a recommendation stops at words — it names a course of action and ends
               there. a referral *routes the patient*: it is a deliberate handoff to a named
               specialty / venue / clinician, carried onward. to call the referrer's act
               "recommend" would collapse the handoff into mere advice and lose the route
               that is the role's whole purpose.
- resolution = keep `refer`; record `recommend` as a forbidden synonym.

### dispute: send — raised 2026-08-14 — status: RESOLVED (keep `refer`)
- raised.by  = mechanic
- claim      = "send" is the plain word for a patient's transfer to another clinician.
- counter    = "send" is too generic — it loses the clinical-handoff sense that `refer`
               carries by convention. a referral is a specific, documented onward route with
               a reason and a target; "send" flattens that to bare transit.
- resolution = keep `refer`; record `send` as a forbidden synonym.

## .evidence

- the role separation is explicit and ordered: diagnostician (what is wrong) → referrer
  (who to see, where) → prescriber (what treatment). `refer` is the referrer's canonical
  verb; it does not diagnose (the diagnostician's act) or treat (the prescriber's act).
- the referrer scope brief bounds the role at "route to the right care" — specialty, venue,
  clinician, preparation — and hands the given urgency across without re-judgement
  (`define.referrer-scope`). `refer.care` names exactly this onward route.
