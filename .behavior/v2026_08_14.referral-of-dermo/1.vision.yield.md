# 1.vision — referral-of-dermo

> ⚠️ **this tool is for informational purposes only and does not constitute medical advice.** analyses are not exhaustive and must not be relied upon for medical decisions. consult a licensed clinician for any health matter — diagnosis, treatment, medication, or emergency care.

---

## .the wish, distilled

a human in the panama city beach, fl area needs a dermatologist — chiefly to sort **moles vs. melanoma**. three real questions sit under that:

1. **self-monitoring** — can i track my own moles at home (weekly phone scans) to catch change early?
2. **access** — which clinicians are actually available? last attempt was a 6-month wait.
3. **virtual vs. physical** — is a virtual visit worse? can i start virtual and upgrade? what do the stats and costs say?

and two build asks fall out of those questions:

- **upgrade the referrer** with an enrouted skill to find *who to go to* (virtual or physical).
- **upgrade the diagnostician** with a melanoma-specific skill, plus **cited** research on (a) virtual-diagnostic success and (b) phone-based melanoma classification via robobrains.

---

## the outcome world

### day-in-the-life

a mole on someone's shoulder "looks a little different." instead of a 6-month blind wait, **two roles do their separate jobs** — each role operates its own skill (wisher's call: the diagnostician and the referrer are the operators, not a patient at a terminal):

- an agent in the **diagnostician** role runs `rhx diagnose.melanoma` — walks the lesion through a guarded, superposition-style assessment: capture the history, enumerate the candidates (benign nevus, dysplastic nevus, melanoma, seborrheic keratosis, lentigo…), disentangle them against literature + the ABCDE / dermoscopy signals, weigh acuity, and land on a **ranked scenario set** with an urgency read — *not* a verdict. every claim traces to a cited source in the route's `accrue/`.
- an agent in the **referrer** role runs `rhx refer.care` — takes the diagnostician's urgency and produces a **referral**: the specialty (dermatology), the venue options (telederm now vs. in-person), and — since there is no sanctioned provider directory (wisher's call) — a **teach-to-search** result: the actual clinician options the referrer found *via a bhrowser search*, each with its real access constraints (wait time, cost, coverage, distance), plus the search method itself so the human can repeat it. a preparation sheet rounds it out (what photos to carry, what to ask).

the two skills stand alone and compose in order — the diagnostician's urgency read is the referrer's input. a physician-role orchestration that chains them into one call is a *possible later convenience*, but out of scope for this first cut; the deliverable is the two role-operated skills.

**the output is two-layered, and the ultimate human reader sees the top layer.** this is the safety hinge (see "who is the reader" under *what is awkward*): the raw clinical rank is the *engine*, not the *interface*. the top layer is a plain-language translation —

> "this mole has a few features worth a professional eye. it is **not an emergency**, but it should be looked at by a dermatologist **soon — within weeks, not months**. here is what to watch for in the meantime, and here is how to get seen — a telederm option is open this week, and two in-person clinics are within reach."

the ranked scenario set, dermoscopy scores, and citations sit *beneath* that, available to a clinician who wants the full detail — but a layperson is never handed a raw differential to self-interpret.

### before / after

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| triage | 6-month wait, no signal on urgency | urgency envelope in an afternoon; know if this is a watch-item or a same-week concern |
| finding care | phone around, hit full books | a shortlist ranked by *walkable* access, virtual + physical |
| self-monitoring | "does this look different?" from memory | a documented cadence + a realistic read on what a phone can and cannot tell you |
| trust | app says "benign," now what? | every claim carries a citation and the not-medical-advice guardrail |

### the "aha" moment

the value clicks when the human sees that **the phone scan is not the diagnosis** — it is a *change-detector and a triage input*, and the two roles route that input to a real clinician on the *shortest path they can actually walk*. the melanoma question stops being "can an app tell me?" and becomes "how do i get the right eyes on this, fast, and what do i track until then?"

## user experience

### usecases & goals

| usecase | role | goal |
|---------|------|------|
| assess a specific mole | diagnostician | ranked differential + acuity envelope, cited — then **translated to plain "how worried / what next"** for the patient |
| decide urgency | diagnostician | emergency / same-week / watch — but a melanoma-flagged lesion never lands in a self-managed "watch"; it defaults to "see a clinician promptly" |
| find a dermatologist | referrer | specialty + venue + clinician options |
| choose virtual vs. in-person | referrer | venue matched to urgency + access + evidence |
| set a self-monitoring cadence | diagnostician (+ preventer boundary) | what to photograph, how often, what "change" warrants escalation |

### contract inputs & outputs

both are **enrouted skills**, mirroring the extant `diagnose.health` contract:

```
rhx refer.care init --at .demo/case=<slug>         # stamp + bind the referral route
rhx diagnose.melanoma init --at .demo/case=<slug>  # stamp + bind the melanoma route
```

- **input**: a `0.seed.md` (the case — lesion description, history, location/access constraints), read verbatim, PII-redacted per `rule.forbid.pii`.
- **output**: per-stone `N.M.phase.step.yield.md` artifacts (case-specific), plus reusable cited facts in `accrue/inventory.of=<topic>.md` (promotable to repo-shared inventory). the final yield is **two-layered** — a plain-language patient summary on top, the cited clinical reasoning beneath.
- **gate**: each stone has a `.guard` (self + peer reviews) before passage — same convention as diagnose.health.
- **binding**: route binds to the current git branch; progress tracked in `passage.jsonl`.

### timeline (referrer route, sketch)

```
intake          → capture the need (specialty implied) + access constraints (cost, distance, wait, language)
venue.enumerate → ER / urgent / primary / specialist / telehealth — which fit the urgency handed over
clinician.find  → candidate providers per venue, virtual + physical
access.rank     → sort by the path the patient can actually walk (wait × cost × distance)
prepare         → what to bring, what to ask, what photos to carry
referral.yield  → the shortlist + prep sheet, disclaimer attached
```

## mental model

### how they'd describe it to a friend

> "one tool figures out how worried to be about the mole and why — with receipts — and the other finds me a real doctor i can actually get in to see, virtual or in person, without the six-month wait."

### analogies

- **diagnostician = a triage nurse with a library**: doesn't hand you a verdict, ranks the possibilities and tells you how fast to move, every claim sourced.
- **referrer = a travel agent for care**: knows the destinations (venues), the routes (virtual/physical), and books the one you can actually reach.
- **phone scan = a smoke detector, not a firefighter**: it flags *change* and raises an alarm; it does not put out the fire.

### their terms vs. ours

| they say | we say |
|----------|--------|
| "is it cancer?" | differential / scenario superposition |
| "how bad is it?" | acuity envelope (emergency / same-day / watch) |
| "find me a doctor" | referral: specialty + venue + clinician |
| "virtual vs. in-person" | venue selection under access constraints |
| "the app that scans moles" | phone-based classification (a triage input, not a diagnosis) |

## evaluation

### how well it solves the goals

- **q2 (access)** — squarely the referrer's job; the role already declares the access dimension (cost, distance, wait, language). strong fit.
- **q3 (virtual vs. physical)** — a venue decision (referrer) informed by cited evidence (diagnostician's accrue). strong fit, *conditional on real citations*.
- **q1 (self-monitoring)** — partial fit. classification is the diagnostician's assay-support; the *cadence/surveillance* leans toward the preventer/monitor role. the honest answer is likely "photograph on a fixed cadence, escalate on ABCDE change — and know the phone cannot rule melanoma out."

### pros

- reuses the proven `diagnose.health` enrouted pattern — low novelty risk.
- respects the already-declared role boundaries (no scope leak between diagnostician and referrer).
- citations-first: the not-medical-advice motto and bhrowser-citation rule are load-bearing, and this vision keeps them central.

### cons / tensions

- the phone-classification ask is precisely where the **evidence is weakest and the liability highest** — the design must resist over-promising.
- referrer clinician-finding needs a **real data source** (directory API? scrape via bhrowser?) — unresolved (see open questions).
- melanoma urgency is real; a "watch" that should have been "same-week" is a costly miss — the acuity envelope must be conservative.

### edgecases & pit of success

| edgecase | how the contract keeps them safe |
|----------|----------------------------------|
| app says "benign," lesion is melanoma | output is never a verdict — always a ranked set + a *conservative* acuity envelope + disclaimer |
| emergency-shaped case (bleeding/ulcerated, rapid change) | motto's escalation carve-out fires: "if this is a medical emergency, call your local emergency number now" |
| no clinicians available at all | referrer surfaces telehealth + widening-radius options rather than a dead end |
| human treats phone scan as diagnosis | mental model + disclaimer framed as smoke-detector, not firefighter, throughout |
| PII in the seed | `rule.forbid.pii` redaction at intake |

## open questions & assumptions

### assumptions made

1. **[confirmed by wisher]** the melanoma skill is a **standalone `diagnose.melanoma`** skill, not a clade/preset of `diagnose.health`.
2. the referrer skill is **general** (`refer.care`) with dermatology as its first case, not a dermo-only skill.
3. self-monitoring cadence is in-scope enough to *address*, even if the deep surveillance mechanism belongs to preventer/monitor.
4. **[confirmed by wisher]** clinician data is **not** from a directory — the referrer runs a **teach-to-search via bhrowser** (live search + repeatable method), so every clinician claim is bhrowser-traceable.
5. **[confirmed by wisher]** the deliverable is the **two skills built**; the three concrete questions (self-monitor feasibility, who's available, virtual-vs-physical stats) are answered *by running the skills*, not as a separate first pass.
6. **[confirmed by wisher]** the **role operates the skill** — an agent in the diagnostician or referrer role runs it; not a patient self-serve, not a human clinician. (this is why the `check.mole` orchestration is out of scope; the two skills stand alone.) the *audience* of the top output layer is still a layperson, which is why the plain-language translation stays.
7. **weekly phone photos are a usable change signal** — even apart from classification, change-detection assumes photos are consistent enough (light, angle, scale) to compare week over week. that fidelity is itself unproven → [research].
8. **[confirmed by wisher] this is both a live personal need and the build fixture.** the case is real *and* the seed the skills are exercised against. so the live-case safe disciplines stay on (escalation carve-out, conservative binary acuity, PII redaction) even as the case doubles as the fixture.

### open questions — triaged

each is tagged **[answered]** (settled now by logic or the repo's own docs), **[research]** (for the bhrowser accrue phase), or **[wisher]** (only the wisher can decide).

- **[answered] referrer scope** — build `refer.care` general with dermatology as the first case, not `refer.dermatology`. the referrer's own scope brief already casts it as a general role that routes to care ("route to the right care"), and `rule.prefer.most-common-denominator` favors the general skill with a narrow first case over a premature narrow skill. settled by the repo's own vocabulary and rules.
- **[answered] self-monitor home** — split by the declared role boundaries: lesion *classification support* is the diagnostician's assay stage; the *cadence / surveillance schedule* is the preventer/monitor's. the diagnostician-scope and referrer-scope briefs already draw these lines, so this needs no wisher input — just correct placement.
- **[answered · wisher] melanoma skill — does it need to exist?** — **yes, a standalone melanoma-specific `diagnose.melanoma` skill** (option a). the wisher chose the dedicated skill over a preset or a bare seed on `diagnose.health`; the melanoma-specific stones (ABCDE, dermoscopy algorithm, Breslow-depth acuity, the binary-acuity collapse) justify their own route.
- **[answered · wisher] clinician source** — **teach-to-search, via bhrowser.** no sanctioned provider directory; the referrer runs an actual bhrowser search to surface real clinician options *and* hands back the repeatable search method. this keeps every clinician claim traceable per `rule.require.bhrowser-citations`.
- **[answered · wisher] answers now, or skills first?** — **skills first; the answers come *from* the skills.** the deliverable is the two skills; the three concrete questions (self-monitor feasibility, local availability, virtual-vs-physical stats) are answered by running them. "done" = the skills built and run to produce those answers.
- **[answered · wisher] who runs the skills?** — **the role does — the diagnostician or the referrer.** an agent operating in each role runs its own skill; not a patient self-serve, not a human clinician at a terminal. this removes the `check.mole` orchestration from scope (the two skills stand alone and compose in order).
- **[answered · wisher] live need or build fixture?** — **both.** a real dermatology need *and* the demo case seed the skills are built against. so the safe-side disciplines of a live case stay on (escalation carve-out, conservative binary acuity, PII redaction) while the case doubles as the fixture that exercises the skills.

### must research externally — [research] (via bhrowser, in the accrue phases — not now)

- **[research]** teledermatology diagnostic concordance vs. in-person, esp. for **pigmented lesions** (where store-and-forward evidence is known to be weaker), and cost deltas.
- **[research]** phone/AI melanoma-classification apps: accuracy, sensitivity/specificity, **FDA/CE regulatory status**, and documented failure modes.
- **[research]** dermatologist availability / wait times in the panama city beach, fl area.
- **[research]** phone-photo *change-detection* fidelity — whether a fixed-capture method makes weekly self-photos comparable enough to trust for change (distinct from classification).

## groundwork

### external research

the wish references external evidence (virtual-diagnostic success, phone melanoma classification) and external services (clinician availability). per `rule.require.bhrowser-citations`, **actual citations must be gathered via bhrowser in the route's accrue phase — not WebSearch/WebFetch, and not now.** so this groundwork is a *sanity check on the vision's assumptions*, flagged for verification, not cited as fact:

- **teledermatology works, but pigmented lesions are the hard case** — general teledermatology concordance is well-supported, yet melanoma/pigmented-lesion telediagnosis is exactly where accuracy drops and in-person dermoscopy still matters. this *validates* the wish's instinct to "start virtual, upgrade if needed" rather than contradicting it. → verify + cite.
- **phone melanoma apps are real but regulated-cautious** — consumer mole-scanning apps are on the market; independent evaluations have historically flagged unreliability, and regulators have scrutinized "diagnostic" claims. a purpose-built FDA-cleared skin-cancer *device* class has emerged recently (device, not a free phone app). the honest vision: phone scan = **change-detector + triage input**, not a classifier the human should trust to rule out melanoma. → verify FDA/CE status + accuracy, cite.
- **derm shortage / long waits in non-metro FL are plausible** — consistent with the human's 6-month experience. → verify local availability at build time.

no assumption here is *known-false*; the one that most needs guarding is the phone-classification capability, which the design already demotes to a triage input.

### internal research

verified against the repo (not assumed):

- **referrer role** — `src/domain.roles/referrer/getReferrerRole.ts` (slug `referrer`, purpose "route to the right care, in the right place"); `briefs/define.referrer-scope.[lesson].md` declares outputs (specialty, venue, test location, preparation) and the access dimension. **skills dir is empty** — this skill is net-new. boot.yml has a commented `skills.ref` slot awaiting the first skill.
- **diagnostician role** — `src/domain.roles/diagnostician/briefs/define.diagnostician-scope.[lesson].md` declares the four stages (intake/differential/assay/triage) and the referrer boundary (which-test vs. where-test). the extant enrouted skill `skills/diagnose.health.sh` stamps a 9-phase superposition route; `diagnose.melanoma` should mirror its contract.
- **enrouted-skill contract** — `diagnose.health.sh`: `init [--at <dir>]`, stamps `.stone` + `.guard` (no `.sh` into the route), creates `accrue/`, binds to branch, fail-fast; output via `diagnose.health/output.sh` (snake header, tree, caduceus footer). `refer.care` and `diagnose.melanoma` should conform.
- **guardrails** — `.agent/repo=.this/role=any/briefs/`: `motto.not-medical-advice.[motto].md` (disclaimer + emergency carve-out; guidance without it = blocker), `rule.require.bhrowser-citations.[rule].md` (WebSearch/WebFetch forbidden as citation source), `rule.require.accrue-research.[rule].md` (generic facts → `accrue/inventory.of=<topic>.md`), `rule.forbid.pii.[rule].md`.
- **vocab to reuse** — `scenario`, `superposition`, `acuity`, `envelope`, `cause.exposure`/`effect.symptom`/`cause.mechanism`/`effect.mechanism`, `clade`, `accrue`; skill naming `<verb>.<noun>` (`diagnose.health` → `diagnose.melanoma`, `refer.care`).

## what is awkward?

- **self-monitoring has no clean home.** classification is diagnostician, cadence/surveillance is preventer/monitor. the wish bundles them under "scan and classify," but the repo splits them. forcing it all into diagnostician would leak scope; splitting it risks a fragmented answer to a single human question. *(needs the wisher's call.)*
- **the referrer's clinician data comes from a live bhrowser search, not a directory.** *(settled by wisher: teach-to-search.)* the referrer browses for real options and hands back the repeatable method. the residual awkwardness: a live search is slower and its results stale, so the deliverable leans on *the method* being durable even when *the list* is not — the human is taught to re-run it, not handed a frozen shortlist.
- **the phone-classification ask fights the not-medical-advice motto.** the human wants "detect & classify"; the honest, guarded answer is "detect change, do not trust classification." delivering that without deflating the wish is a genuine tension the copy must handle with care.
- **melanoma acuity is asymmetric.** the cost of under-calling urgency (missed melanoma) dwarfs over-calling. the acuity envelope must lean conservative, which may frustrate a human who wanted reassurance, not a nudge toward a clinic. **sharper:** for a *melanoma-suspicious* lesion the "watch" tier is itself a hazard — a false "watch" reads as reassurance. the safe default for any melanoma-flagged lesion is "have a clinician look, promptly," not a self-managed watch window. **sharper still:** for the melanoma case the acuity likely collapses to near-**binary** — emergency now (bleeding, ulcerated, fast change) vs. see-a-clinician-soon — rather than the three-tier grade borrowed from `diagnose.health`; the tool should not claim a finer urgency resolution than it can safely own for a possible cancer.
- **operator vs. reader — now split cleanly.** *(settled by wisher: the role operates.)* the **operator** is an agent in the diagnostician or referrer role, not a patient. the **reader** of the top output layer is still a layperson. so the two-layer output stands for a different reason than first thought: not because a patient *runs* the tool, but because the plain-language layer is what a patient ultimately *reads*. the residual awkwardness: the role must know to emit the layperson layer even though a role, not a person, invoked it.
- **two skills, one wish.** the human asked for referrer *and* diagnostician upgrades plus citations — three deliverables. a crisp role boundary under one behavior is a coordination cost. **on sequence:** the citations are not a third peer deliverable — they are the *foundation both skills stand on* (the melanoma skill's assay stones and the referrer's virtual-vs-physical rank both consume them). so the natural build order is **accrue-inventories first, skills second**, not three parallel tracks.
- **does referral need the heavy enrouted machinery?** the wish says "enrouted skill," and the vision mirrors `diagnose.health`'s multi-stone-with-guards pattern. but a referral ("who + where") may be lighter than a diagnosis (superposition logic). the full per-stone self+peer-review apparatus could be overkill for a referral task. the wisher asked for enrouted, so the vision honors it — but a lighter single-pass referral skill is a live alternative worth a call-out. *(flagged for the wisher; clean rework.)*
